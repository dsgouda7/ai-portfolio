Chapter 3: The Wedding Ceremony

The morning of Wei Lian's wedding dawned clear and bright, an auspicious sign according to the fortuneteller her mother had consulted. The Wei household had been in a state of controlled chaos for days, with servants rushing to complete final preparations while family members arrived from throughout the province. The compound smelled of incense and cooking food, of fresh flowers and new silk.

Wei Lian woke before dawn, her last morning as a daughter of the Wei household. Plum Blossom had already prepared the bathing room, filling the wooden tub with hot water scented with jasmine and rose petals. The ritual bath would cleanse her of her old life, preparing her for the new.

"You must soak until your skin is soft," Plum Blossom instructed, fussing over the water temperature. The maid had been with Wei Lian since childhood, and her eyes were suspiciously bright. "Lady Wei says every detail must be perfect."

Wei Lian lowered herself into the steaming water, feeling the fragrant heat seep into her muscles. Through the latticed window, she could see the morning sky turning from gray to pink to gold. In a few hours, she would leave this compound forever, carried in a red sedan chair to the Zhang family home where she would be received as a bride.

She tried to catalog her feelings. Nervousness, certainly. Anticipation. A strange sense of unreality, as if this were happening to someone else. And beneath it all, a stubborn core of determination. She would be a good wife. She would honor her family. But she would also, somehow, find a way to remain herself.

After the bath, the real preparations began. Lady Wei supervised with military precision as three maids worked on Wei Lian's appearance. Her hair was combed with a special comb dipped in perfumed oil, then arranged in the elaborate style prescribed for brides, with dozens of hairpins securing the complex coils and loops. Her face was powdered until it glowed like white jade, her lips painted red, her eyebrows darkened and shaped into perfect arches.

The bridal robes were a masterpiece of the silk-maker's art, layer upon layer of the finest fabric in shades of red and gold, embroidered with phoenixes and peonies, symbols of feminine grace and prosperity. The weight of them was considerable, and Wei Lian felt herself transforming under the layers, becoming less herself and more the idealized image of a bride.

"Beautiful," Lady Wei whispered when the transformation was complete. She blinked rapidly, fighting tears. "My daughter, a bride. It seems impossible that you were just a small child, learning to write your characters."

Wei Lian wanted to embrace her mother but dared not disturb the elaborate arrangements. "Thank you, Mother. For everything."

Lady Wei's composure cracked slightly. "I have been hard on you sometimes. Too critical, perhaps. But it was only to prepare you for the world. A woman must be strong and clever to navigate the life fate gives us."

"I know, Mother."

"Zhang Ming seems a good man. But remember, even good men can disappoint. Keep your grandmother's jade close, and remember that you are a daughter of the Wei family. That means something. It means you have steel in your spine, even if you must hide it beneath silk."

Before Wei Lian could respond, servants announced that the Zhang family's procession was approaching. The carefully choreographed rituals of the wedding ceremony were beginning.

The next hours passed in a blur of ceremony and protocol. Zhang Ming arrived at the Wei compound with a procession of musicians, servants, and family members, all dressed in their finest robes. Wei Lian, hidden behind a red veil, was led out to the ancestral hall where she bowed to her family's ancestors for the last time as an unmarried woman.

Wei Chen placed a hand on her head in blessing, his voice gruff with emotion. "Go with our love and our pride, daughter. Honor your ancestors and your new family."

Then she was lifted into the red sedan chair, the veil obscuring her view of the world. The chair swayed as the bearers lifted it, and the procession began its journey through the streets of Chang'an to the Zhang compound on the western side of the city.

Through the silk veil, Wei Lian caught fragmentary glimpses of the route: curious onlookers lining the streets, children running alongside the procession, the towering gates of the imperial district. The musicians played traditional wedding melodies on flutes and drums, announcing to all that a marriage was taking place. The journey felt both endless and too brief.

When the sedan chair finally stopped and she was helped down, Wei Lian found herself in a courtyard larger and more austere than her family's compound. The Zhang family valued scholarly restraint over merchant opulence, and their home reflected this philosophy. The gardens were simple but elegant, the buildings well-maintained but not ostentatious.

Lady Zhang waited at the entrance to the main hall, her severe face composed. "Welcome, daughter-in-law. Enter your new home."

Wei Lian bowed deeply, still veiled, and was led into the hall where the marriage ceremony would be completed. Zhang Ming waited there, dressed in formal robes of deep blue silk embroidered with gold thread. She could not see his face clearly through the veil, but she sensed his nervousness matching her own.

The ceremony proceeded according to ancient custom. They bowed together to Heaven and Earth, acknowledging the cosmic forces that governed all life. They bowed to Zhang Ming's ancestors, Wei Lian formally entering their lineage. They bowed to Lady Zhang and the assembled family elders, receiving their blessings. And finally, they bowed to each other, husband and wife, their fates now intertwined.

The veil was lifted, and Wei Lian saw Zhang Ming's face clearly for the first time that day. He looked pale and serious, but when their eyes met, he gave her the smallest of reassuring smiles. It was enough. She felt some of her nervousness ease.

The wedding feast was lavish, a display of both families' prosperity and status. Long tables filled the Zhang compound's main hall and spilled out into the courtyards. Guests from the city's elite merchant and scholarly communities had been invited, along with family members from distant provinces. The food was abundant and exquisite: whole roasted pigs, steamed fish from the Yellow River, delicacies from across the empire brought by Silk Road traders.

Wei Lian and Zhang Ming sat at the place of honor, accepting congratulations and toasts from the assembled guests. The wine flowed freely, and the atmosphere grew increasingly festive as the afternoon wore on. Musicians played, acrobats performed, and poets composed impromptu verses celebrating the union.

Through it all, Wei Lian maintained the serene composure expected of a bride, but she was intensely aware of the man beside her. Zhang Ming spoke little, also constrained by ceremony, but occasionally their hands would brush as they reached for teacups or rice wine, and each touch was like a small electric shock.

As evening approached, the married couple was finally permitted to retire to the bridal chamber, a room that had been prepared with red candles, fresh flowers, and the finest furniture the Zhang household possessed. Lady Zhang personally escorted them to the threshold and gave her son a brief, awkward pat on the shoulder before departing.

The door closed behind them, and for the first time, Wei Lian and Zhang Ming were truly alone.

The silence stretched between them, suddenly awkward. Zhang Ming cleared his throat. "I suppose we should... that is... Would you like some tea? There is a pot here."

Wei Lian felt laughter bubble up unexpectedly at his nervousness. "Yes, thank you. Tea would be welcome."

He poured with slightly shaky hands, the sound of liquid filling cups unnaturally loud in the quiet room. They sat across from each other at a small table, sipping tea and avoiding direct eye contact like shy children rather than a newly married couple.

"I must confess," Zhang Ming finally said, "that I am not entirely certain what is expected now. The ritual aspects were clear enough, but the actual... that is, the practical matter of..."

"Perhaps," Wei Lian interrupted gently, "we might simply talk first? We have written many letters, but we have barely spoken to each other directly."

Relief flooded Zhang Ming's face. "Yes. Yes, that would be... I would like that very much."

And so they talked, the formality of the wedding day gradually dissolving as they spoke of inconsequential things: the music at the feast, the absurd hat worn by one of the elderly relatives, the quality of the wine. Slowly, the people they had been in their letters emerged from behind the ceremonial masks.

"I was terrified this morning," Zhang Ming admitted. "I kept forgetting the order of the bows during the ceremony."

"I did not notice," Wei Lian assured him. "I was too focused on not tripping over these robes. They must weigh as much as I do."

He laughed, a genuine sound of amusement. "You should see the official court robes. Even more elaborate, and one is expected to move through complex protocols without stumbling."

"You have been to court?"

"Only twice, for minor ceremonial occasions. But I received word yesterday that I have been assigned to the Ministry of Rites, as we hoped. We will remain in Chang'an, at least for now."

Wei Lian felt a surge of relief. She had feared being sent to some distant province, far from her family. "That is good news."

"I thought so. Though my mother was hoping for a posting to Luoyang, where her family is from." Zhang Ming paused. "Wei Lian, I want you to know... I do not expect this marriage to follow the traditional pattern in all ways. My mother is formidable, and she will have opinions about how our household should be run. But you are my wife, not her servant. I hope we can be partners, as I wrote in my letters."

"That is my hope as well."

"Good." He smiled, and Wei Lian thought again that while he was not conventionally handsome, his face had a pleasant openness that was more valuable than mere beauty. "Now, as for tonight... I think we are both exhausted from the ceremony and the feast. Perhaps we might simply rest? There will be time enough for... other matters."

Wei Lian felt profound gratitude for his consideration. She had been steeling herself for the wedding night, knowing what was expected, but the thought of postponing that intimacy was immensely welcome. "I would appreciate that."

They prepared for sleep with the awkwardness of two people unused to sharing space. The bridal bed was large enough for two, piled high with embroidered quilts and silk pillows. They lay down side by side, a careful distance between them, and for a while neither spoke.

Then Zhang Ming's voice came softly through the darkness. "Wei Lian?"

"Yes?"

"I am glad it was you. When my mother first proposed this marriage, I was simply resigned to accepting whomever she chose. But then I met you, and read your letters, and I began to hope that perhaps marriage might be more than mere duty. That perhaps we might even be happy."

Wei Lian felt her throat tighten with unexpected emotion. "I hope so too."

"Goodnight, wife."

"Goodnight, husband."

She lay awake long after Zhang Ming's breathing had evened into sleep, listening to the unfamiliar sounds of her new home, feeling the weight of the jade pendant that hung around her neck beneath her sleeping robe. Her old life had ended today. A new one was beginning.

What it would hold, she did not know. But lying there in the darkness beside her new husband, Wei Lian felt something she had not expected to feel: a quiet sense of possibility. The door had closed on her life as Wei Chen's daughter. But perhaps another door was opening onto something unexpected.

She drifted into sleep with that thought, her hand curled around her grandmother's jade pendant, Zhang Ming's steady breathing a counterpoint to her own.

The next morning, Wei Lian woke to find herself alone in the bridal chamber. Sunlight streamed through the latticed windows, and she could hear the sounds of the Zhang household beginning its daily routine. For a moment, disoriented, she could not remember where she was. Then memory returned with the weight of her new reality.

She was Zhang Wei Lian now, a married woman, a member of the Zhang household. Her old name was not entirely lost, but it was transformed, subsumed into her husband's family identity.

A soft knock at the door was followed by the entrance of a middle-aged maid. "Good morning, young mistress. I am Autumn Moon, assigned to serve you. Lady Zhang requests your presence in the ancestral hall when you are ready."

Wei Lian rose and allowed Autumn Moon to help her dress in robes more practical than the elaborate wedding garments. The maid was efficient but reserved, her face giving away nothing of her thoughts about the new bride.

The ancestral hall was a solemn space, dominated by tablets commemorating generations of Zhang family members. Lady Zhang waited there, dressed in formal robes, her expression stern.

"You will pay respects to your husband's ancestors each morning," she instructed as Wei Lian approached. "This is the first duty of a daughter-in-law. You will also oversee the household offerings and ensure the family shrines are properly maintained."

Wei Lian bowed and performed the ritual obeisances, feeling the weight of tradition and expectation settling onto her shoulders like a physical burden. She was no longer an individual but a link in a chain stretching back through generations and forward into an unknown future.

"My son speaks highly of your education," Lady Zhang continued as they left the hall. "This is well enough, but remember that learning is secondary to a wife's primary duties. You will oversee the household accounts, manage the servants, and in time, produce heirs. These are the measures by which you will be judged."

"I understand, honorable mother-in-law."

"Good. You may call me Mother Zhang, as is proper. Come, I will show you the household and explain your responsibilities."

The morning passed in a blur of instruction and inspection. The Zhang compound was larger than the Wei family home but less luxuriously appointed. There were servants to direct, accounts to review, supplies to monitor. The kitchen needed overseeing, the gardens required maintenance, the household shrines demanded daily attention.

Lady Zhang watched everything with a critical eye, correcting Wei Lian's every minor mistake, establishing from the first day that she remained the true power in the household. Wei Lian absorbed it all, keeping her face pleasant and her demeanor respectful, but inside she felt exhaustion creeping in.

By midday, when she was finally permitted to retire to the quarters she would share with Zhang Ming, Wei Lian felt as if she had aged years in a single morning. She sank onto a cushioned bench and closed her eyes.

"Was my mother very difficult?" Zhang Ming's voice made her eyes snap open. He stood in the doorway, concern evident on his face.

"She was... thorough," Wei Lian said diplomatically.

Zhang Ming entered and closed the door behind him. "I apologize. She means well, but she finds it hard to relinquish control. Give her time. Once she sees you are capable, she will ease somewhat."

"I hope you are right."

"I have something for you." He held out a small wooden box. "A wedding gift, though I should have given it yesterday."

Wei Lian opened the box to find a set of writing brushes, each one crafted from the finest materials, the handles carved from jade and sandalwood. "They are beautiful."

"I noticed that you love calligraphy. I thought... I know that marriage brings many duties, but I hope you will still find time for the pursuits you enjoy. Our home should have room for learning as well as household management."

Wei Lian looked up at him, feeling tears prick her eyes. "Thank you. This means more than you know."

He smiled, that gentle expression that was becoming familiar. "We are partners, remember? In all things, I hope."

As Wei Lian held the beautiful brushes, feeling their perfect weight and balance, she thought that perhaps this marriage might indeed be bearable. Perhaps, within its constraints, she might find unexpected freedoms. Perhaps Zhang Ming's vision of partnership was not merely pleasant words but a genuine possibility.

Only time would tell. But for now, on her first full day as a married woman, Wei Lian allowed herself to believe in that possibility. The door to her old life had closed. But the door to this new life might yet open onto interesting places.

She set the brushes carefully aside and looked at her husband. "Thank you, Zhang Ming. For the gift, and for your kindness."

"It is easy to be kind to you," he replied simply. And then, with a slight bow, he departed to attend to his duties at the Ministry of Rites, leaving Wei Lian alone with her thoughts and the first tendrils of hope.

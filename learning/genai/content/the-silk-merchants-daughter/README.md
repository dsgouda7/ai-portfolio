# The Silk Merchant's Daughter
## An Original Historical Fiction Novel

### Synopsis

Set during the Tang Dynasty (circa 730-750 CE), "The Silk Merchant's Daughter" follows Wei Lian, a seventeen-year-old merchant's daughter in Chang'an, as she navigates an arranged marriage and embarks on an extraordinary journey along the Silk Road.

When Wei Lian marries Zhang Ming, a promising scholar-official, she expects a conventional life managing a household. Instead, Zhang Ming invites her to join an imperial delegation traveling the Silk Road to assess trade conditions in the western regions. Against social convention and her mother-in-law's initial objections, Wei Lian seizes this unprecedented opportunity.

The journey transforms Wei Lian from a sheltered young woman into a capable consultant on trade matters. She survives bandit attacks, crosses treacherous mountain passes, navigates diverse cultures from Dunhuang to Khotan to Kashgar and finally to the legendary city of Samarkand. Along the way, she discovers her own strength, develops expertise in commercial assessment, and earns respect from the delegation's leadership.

Returning to Chang'an after more than a year of travel, Wei Lian must reconcile her transformed self with the constraints of Tang Dynasty society. She establishes herself as an informal consultant on Silk Road trade—an unprecedented role for a woman—while fulfilling her duties as wife, daughter-in-law, and eventually mother. The novel concludes with Wei Lian having carved out space for her ambitions within the social structures of her time, becoming a quiet pioneer for women's intellectual and professional participation.

"The Silk Merchant's Daughter" explores themes of gender roles, cultural exchange, personal transformation, and the balance between tradition and innovation. It depicts the Silk Road not just as a trade route but as a meeting place of civilizations, where boundaries—both geographic and personal—become fluid and negotiable.

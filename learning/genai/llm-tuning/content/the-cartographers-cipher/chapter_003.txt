Chapter 3: The Professor

Dr. Rebecca Kahale's office at the university overlooked a courtyard filled with cherry trees. Elena had called ahead, explaining she was investigating Adelaide Thorne's murder and needed to discuss the founding families' history. Dr. Kahale had agreed to meet immediately.

She was in her late forties, with long dark hair streaked with gray and eyes that assessed Elena with sharp intelligence. Her office walls were covered with maps, historical photographs, and framed documents. One entire shelf held books about indigenous land rights and colonial history.

"Detective Chen called me this morning," Dr. Kahale said after they had settled into chairs. "He said Adelaide's murder might be connected to the founding families. I told him the same thing I'll tell you. My family has been fighting the consequences of the city's founding for five generations. We're victims, not perpetrators."

"I'm not suggesting otherwise," Elena said carefully. "In fact, the evidence I've found suggests your family was specifically targeted by the fraud. I need to understand what happened."

Dr. Kahale's expression softened slightly. "Then you've actually looked at the real history, not the sanitized version they teach in schools."

"I've spent the morning reading Thomas Ashmont's private papers. He and the other founding families used falsified surveys to cheat your family out of land. They bribed federal officials, created fake documentation, and paid Chief Samuel Kahale far less than the land was worth, assuming they paid him anything beyond token compensation."

"They paid him in worthless scrip and broken promises. My great-great-grandfather was illiterate in English, though he was fluent in three indigenous languages and knew every inch of this territory. The 'surveyors' showed him maps he couldn't read and told him he was selling fifty acres. They actually took nearly three hundred. By the time he understood what had happened, the incorporation was complete and federal oversight had ended. He spent the rest of his life trying to get justice."

"Did he ever succeed?"

"Marginally. He got back about sixty acres through a lawsuit in the eighteen nineties, land that's still in our family. But the bulk of what was stolen, the prime waterfront property, the timber lands, the sites where the city's first businesses were built, all of that stayed with the founding families. My family has fought legal battles every generation, but the courts have consistently ruled that the original transactions, however fraudulent, are too old to challenge."

Elena pulled out her phone and showed Dr. Kahale the photographs from the Ashmont archives. "What if there was proof? Documented evidence of the fraud, a map showing the true boundaries and ownership?"

Dr. Kahale examined the images, her hands trembling slightly as she scrolled through them. "Where did you get these?"

"The Ashmont Trust's private archives. Victoria Ashmont gave me access."

"Victoria gave you these? Why would she undermine her own family's fortune?"

"Because Adelaide Thorne called her yesterday morning and told her she had found evidence of the fraud. Victoria wants the truth, regardless of the cost."

Dr. Kahale set down the phone and stood, pacing to the window. "Do you understand what this means? If these documents were authenticated and made public, my family could file new lawsuits challenging the founding property transfers. We could potentially claim damages in the hundreds of millions. So could others who can prove they were cheated. The entire legal foundation of the city could collapse."

"Which gives people a strong motive to keep these documents hidden."

"It also gives my family a strong motive to reveal them. Did Detective Chen mention that?"

"Should he have?"

Dr. Kahale turned back to face her. "I'm not naive, Miss Voss. I know how investigations work. Someone in my family could have killed Adelaide to get these documents, to finally have the proof we've needed for generations. We didn't do it, but we had motive and opportunity."

"Where were you yesterday between two and six PM?"

"Teaching a seminar on colonial land appropriation. Twenty students can verify my presence. My brother Thomas, named after our great-great-grandfather, was at work in Seattle. My sister Maria was at her law office downtown, also with witnesses. We're not killers, Miss Voss. We're survivors of a historical injustice, trying to preserve our heritage and get recognition of what was stolen from us."

Elena believed her, but belief wasn't evidence. "Did Adelaide contact you before her death?"

"No. I hadn't spoken to her in months. We served together on the historical society board, but that was the extent of our relationship."

"Did you know she was researching the founding families?"

"Half the historians in this city are always researching the founding families. It's one of our defining narratives. I didn't know her research had turned up anything significant."

Elena showed her more photographs, including Thomas Ashmont's letter about J. Mordecai's "permanent removal."

Dr. Kahale's expression darkened. "So it was murder even then. The conspiracy didn't just steal land, it killed to protect itself."

"And it's still killing. Which means the corruption is ongoing. Someone alive today is profiting from the fraud and willing to commit murder to keep it hidden."

"Do you have any suspects?"

"Everyone descended from the founding families is a potential suspect. Including you."

Dr. Kahale's laugh was bitter. "Except my family doesn't profit from the fraud. We're the ones who lost everything. If anyone benefits from keeping this buried, it's the Ashmonts, the Blackwells, the Winters, whoever inherited the Mordecai fortune."

"Tell me about the Mordecai family. They disappeared from the historical record around nineteen hundred."

"They did more than disappear. They were erased. I've researched them for years. J. Mordecai was a financier from Boston, representing an eastern investment syndicate. He provided the bulk of the capital for the city's founding. Then, around eighteen eighty-eight, all records of him stop. No death certificate, no estate proceedings, no family obituaries. It's as if he never existed."

"Thomas Ashmont's letters suggest he was murdered when he tried to blackmail the other founding families."

"That would explain the erasure. If they killed him, they'd want to eliminate any evidence of his existence, any claims his heirs might make. But that raises a question. What happened to his money? The Mordecai fortune was substantial. It didn't just vanish."

Elena thought about the letters, the references to the eastern syndicate being compensated. "Maybe it was absorbed by the other families. Divided as spoils."

"Maybe. Or maybe his heirs exist somewhere, not knowing their history, not knowing they were cheated out of a fortune. That would make them victims too."

"Or it would give them a motive for revenge. If someone recently discovered they were a Mordecai heir and learned they'd been cheated for generations, they might want justice. Or vengeance."

Dr. Kahale considered this. "You think the killer might be a Mordecai descendant?"

"I think I need to find out if any Mordecai descendants exist. And I think I need to find that map. According to Ashmont's letters, it shows the true property boundaries and ownership. With that map, we could trace the stolen properties forward to their current owners. We could identify everyone who's currently benefiting from the fraud."

"And one of those people is probably our killer."

"Exactly."

Dr. Kahale moved to her desk and pulled out a folder. "I may be able to help you. I've been researching the founding families for a book I'm writing about indigenous land rights in the Pacific Northwest. I have copies of documents from various archives, some that aren't publicly accessible. If Adelaide was following a trail of clues to find the map, I might be able to help you reconstruct her research."

She spread papers across her desk. Property records, survey maps, correspondence, photographs. "The key to finding the map is understanding how Thomas Ashmont thought. He was a surveyor, a systematic thinker. If he hid something, he would have left clues that another surveyor could follow. He couldn't resist the puzzle."

Elena studied the documents. "He mentioned in his letters that the map and 'its guardians' were secure. That sounds like he didn't just hide it in a safe deposit box. He created some kind of system."

"Agreed. And he told only Elijah Thorne where to find it. The Thorne family was trusted with the secret. Adelaide would have inherited that knowledge, or at least clues to it."

"She mentioned in her call to Victoria Ashmont that she'd found something in her family's papers. Something that scared her enough to hire outside help."

Dr. Kahale pulled out another document. "Look at this. It's Elijah Thorne's will, probated in nineteen twenty-three. He left his house and personal effects to his son, but he established a trust for the maintenance of 'the family archives and certain historical materials held in perpetual guardianship.' That trust still exists. It funds the Thorne Collection at the city's historical museum."

"So the map might be in the museum?"

"It's possible. Though if it were that obvious, someone would have found it decades ago. More likely, Elijah hid it within the collection in a way that would only be obvious to his descendants. A book with a false interior, a map tube with a false bottom, something disguised as ordinary historical material."

Elena felt a surge of excitement. "Adelaide was a librarian before she retired. She would have known every item in the Thorne Collection. If there were hidden materials, she would be the one to find them."

"But did she find them before she was killed? Or did she just find clues pointing toward them?"

"That's what I need to determine. Can you get me access to the Thorne Collection?"

"I can do better than that. The curator, Sophia Winters, is a friend and colleague. I'll call her now and arrange for you to examine the collection this afternoon."

While Dr. Kahale made the call, Elena examined the documents spread across the desk. She found herself drawn to an old photograph, faded sepia tones showing a group of men in nineteenth-century formal wear. The caption identified them as the founding families, photographed at the city's incorporation ceremony in July eighteen seventy-nine.

Six men stood in a line. Thomas Ashmont, tall and bearded, in the center. Elijah Thorne, thin and scholarly, to his right. Malcolm Blackwell, prosperous and stern, to his left. A heavy-set man labeled Samuel Kahale, wearing traditional clothing mixed with western garments. A younger man, elegant and watchful, identified as J. Mordecai. And finally, a nondescript figure at the edge of the frame, partially in shadow, labeled simply as Winters.

Elena had seen paintings and later photographs of most of these men, but this was the only image she'd found that included J. Mordecai. She studied his face, looking for some indication of the fate that would befall him nine years later.

Dr. Kahale finished her call. "Sophia can see you at two o'clock. The museum archives are in the basement. She'll have the Thorne Collection ready for your examination."

"Thank you. One more question. Do you know anything about the Winters family? They seem to be the most obscure of the founding families."

"Deliberately so, I think. The Winters family made their fortune and then withdrew from public life. Unlike the Ashmonts, who put their name on everything, or the Blackwells, who stayed in business, the Winters simply faded away. The last prominent member of the family was Sophia's grandfather, who died in the nineteen sixties. Her father was a recluse, and she's the only surviving heir."

"Is she friendly with the other founding families?"

"Friendly might be too strong a word. Cordial. Professional. She's dedicated to historical preservation, which sometimes brings her into conflict with development interests. Marcus Blackwell particularly dislikes her because she's blocked several of his projects on historical grounds."

Elena made notes. Another connection, another tension. "Does she know about the fraud?"

"I don't think so. She's never mentioned it to me, and we've had many conversations about the founding period. If she knew her family had been involved in land theft and murder, I think she would have said something. Sophia has a strong ethical sense."

"Or a strong sense of self-preservation. If the fraud is exposed, the Winters fortune might be challenged just like the others."

Dr. Kahale's expression was troubled. "I want to believe Sophia is innocent in this. She's been helpful to my research, supportive of indigenous history preservation. But you're right. Everyone descended from those families has something to lose."

Elena gathered her notes and stood. "I'll talk to her this afternoon. In the meantime, if you think of anything else that might help me find that map, call me immediately. And Dr. Kahale, be careful. Whoever killed Adelaide might view you as a threat too."

"Because I have motive to expose the fraud?"

"Because you have the historical knowledge to help find the map. You're valuable as an asset or dangerous as a threat. Either way, you're not safe."

Dr. Kahale walked her to the door. "Miss Voss, when you find that map, what will you do with it? Will you make it public immediately?"

"I'll turn it over to the authorities and let them decide. My job is to solve Adelaide's murder and uncover the truth. What the city does with that truth is beyond my scope."

"But you must have an opinion. Should the victims of the fraud be compensated? Should the descendants of the perpetrators be punished for their ancestors' crimes? Should the entire legal foundation of the city be challenged?"

Elena met her eyes. "I think the truth matters more than comfort or convenience. I think justice delayed is still justice. And I think Adelaide Thorne died trying to expose a wrong that's been festering for nearly a hundred and fifty years. The least we can do is finish what she started."

She left the university and drove to the waterfront, parking near the site of the original city incorporation. A historical marker commemorated the event, listing the founding families and praising their vision and civic virtue.

Someone had spray-painted a single word across the bronze plaque: LIES.

Elena photographed it, wondering if it was recent vandalism or old graffiti that maintenance hadn't yet cleaned. Either way, it felt prophetic.

Her phone buzzed with a text from Detective Chen. "Got the archive inventory. Adelaide requested 14 boxes over the past month. Most focused on 1879-1890 period. Also requested the architectural plans for the first city hall, built 1881. Seemed interested in the building's foundation."

The foundation. Thomas Ashmont had written about "the foundation" holding. Was he being literal? Had he hidden the map in the foundation of the city hall building?

Elena typed back: "Does the original city hall still exist?"

Chen's response came quickly: "Demolished in 1952. Site is now a parking garage."

So much for that theory. Unless the map had been recovered during demolition and moved elsewhere. Or unless Ashmont had meant a different kind of foundation entirely.

She checked the time. One-thirty. She had thirty minutes before her meeting with Sophia Winters at the museum. Enough time to grab coffee and review her notes.

The case was expanding faster than she could track. Six founding families, at least four with living descendants. A conspiracy of fraud and murder dating back nearly a century and a half. A hidden map that proved everything. And someone willing to kill in the present day to keep the past buried.

Somewhere in this tangle was Adelaide's killer. Someone with the means, motive, and opportunity to commit murder. Someone who knew Adelaide was getting close to finding the map and had to stop her.

The question was who. Victoria Ashmont seemed genuinely shocked by the revelations, but that could be an act. Marcus Blackwell had a reputation for ruthlessness, though no direct evidence of violence. Dr. Rebecca Kahale had an alibi but a clear motive. Sophia Winters was still an unknown quantity.

And then there was the shadow of the Mordecai family, erased from history but possibly not extinct. If a Mordecai heir existed and had discovered the truth, they would have the strongest motive of all for revenge.

Elena finished her coffee and drove to the museum. Time to examine the Thorne Collection and see if Adelaide had left any clues about where the map was hidden.

Or if she had already found it before she died.

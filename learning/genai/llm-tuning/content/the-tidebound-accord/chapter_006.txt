Chapter 6: Into the Abyss

Finding a ship proved harder than expected. With the pillars rising and the city in chaos, most captains were either refusing to leave port or heading directly away from the Trench at maximum speed. None wanted to hire themselves out to a disgraced scholar and an unbound girl for a journey to the most dangerous waters in Vaetheris.

"We'll have to steal a boat," Davin said grimly after their fifth rejection.

"I'm not a thief," Kerra protested.

"You'd rather be dead? Because that's the alternative if we stay here much longer."

Before Kerra could respond, a voice cut through their argument. "I might have a solution to your problem."

They turned to find a woman watching them from the shadow of a nearby warehouse. She was perhaps forty, her skin bearing the slight green-blue tint of a Deepwater weaver, though less pronounced than most. Her eyes were sharp and calculating, and she wore the practical clothes of a ship's officer—worn leather vest, salt-stained trousers, a knife at her belt that looked well-used.

"Who are you?" Davin asked warily.

"Captain Lysse Thornwind. Originally from the Skyward Holds before I decided I preferred the sea to the mountains. I couldn't help but overhear that you need passage to the Trench." She stepped into the light, revealing a face that might have been beautiful if not for the long scar running from her left temple to her jaw. "Interesting destination, given that everyone else is running from it."

"Why are you interested in our business?" Kerra asked, her hand instinctively moving toward the concealed pocket where she kept the stone-tide essence vial Davin had given her.

"Because I've been tracking the same tide-vein sabotage you have. Because I've spent three years trying to convince my former colleagues in the Holds that something catastrophic is coming and being ignored for my trouble. And because—" Lysse's gaze fixed on Kerra with uncomfortable intensity "—I can sense what you are, girl. You're a resonant, or I've lost all my wind-weaver instincts. And if a resonant is heading toward the Trench, that means things are far worse than even I feared."

Davin and Kerra exchanged glances. "You could be Hollowed," Davin said flatly. "This could be a trap."

"It could be. But if I wanted to capture you, I wouldn't do it by offering you my ship in full view of half the city's dock workers." Lysse gestured to a sleek two-masted vessel moored nearby. "That's the Stormrunner. Fast, well-armed, and provisioned for long voyages. I've been preparing to make a run to the Trench myself, hoping to find evidence of whatever's causing the destabilizations. Seems like we have common goals."

"What do you get out of helping us?" Kerra asked.

"The satisfaction of being right, for one. The chance to actually do something useful before everything falls apart, for another. And—" Lysse's expression grew serious "—the opportunity to possibly prevent a war between the Enclaves and Holds that's currently looking inevitable. If we can prove that a third party is responsible for the sabotage, maybe we can redirect all that misplaced anger toward the real enemy."

It was a compelling argument, but Kerra remained suspicious. Then again, they didn't exactly have better options.

"Show us your ship," Davin said finally. "Let us inspect it, check your credentials, meet your crew. If everything checks out, we'll discuss terms."

"Fair enough. Follow me."

The Stormrunner was impressive—sleek hulled and clearly built for speed, with lines that suggested both Deepwater and Skyward design influences. Her crew numbered eight, all of them experienced sailors who regarded Kerra and Davin with curiosity but no hostility.

"Picked them all myself," Lysse explained as they toured the vessel. "Every one of them has lost someone or something to the tide-vein instability. They're motivated, loyal, and they know this voyage might be one-way. No illusions about the dangers we're facing."

Davin examined the ship's charts while Kerra used her tide-sense to check for any hostile void-weavings or other signs of sabotage. She found nothing suspicious—just a well-maintained vessel prepared for serious work.

"We can pay," Davin said finally, pulling out a heavy purse. "Not a fortune, but enough to compensate your crew for the risk."

"Keep your money. I'm not doing this for profit." Lysse studied them both. "But I need honesty from you. What exactly are we sailing toward? What's in the Trench that's so important?"

Kerra and Davin shared another look. Finally, Davin sighed. "Tell her. If we're trusting her with our lives, she deserves to know what she's getting into."

So Kerra told her. Everything. The Hollow King, the Abyssal Rift, the ancient seal failing, the Hollowed working to free their master. Her own nature as a resonant, the training with Davin, the network of void-weavings draining the tide-veins. As she spoke, Lysse's expression shifted from skepticism to concern to grim acceptance.

"Well," Lysse said when Kerra finished. "That's significantly worse than I expected. I was just worried about environmental collapse, not the literal end of the world." She laughed, but there was no humor in it. "All right. We leave within the hour. If we're going to stop an ancient evil from destroying everything, we might as well get started."

True to her word, the Stormrunner cast off as the sun began its descent toward evening. Kerra stood at the rail and watched Deepwater Crossing recede behind them, the pillars still glowing in their circle around the city like the bars of an enormous cage.

"You've never been to the deep waters before," Lysse observed, joining her at the rail. "I can tell by how you're standing. People who've sailed the Trench develop a different stance—lower center of gravity, always ready for the ship to roll hard in any direction."

"I've sailed plenty," Kerra said defensively. "Just not that far south."

"The deep is different. The currents run strange, the water itself feels heavier, and there are things down there that even the Deepwater folk avoid. Old things, from before the tides were divided." Lysse's scarred face was thoughtful in the fading light. "I've made the journey twice before. Lost crew both times, though we did make it back. This trip, though... this one might not have a return route."

"Then why are you doing it?"

"Because someone has to. Because I saw this coming three years ago and spent all that time trying to warn people, and now it's too late for warnings. Action is all that's left." Lysse turned to face her fully. "You're young, Kerra. Too young for this weight. But I've seen how you move, how you control yourself around the tides even when you think no one's watching. You've got the raw power to make a difference. Question is, do you have the will?"

"I don't know," Kerra admitted. "I'm terrified I'll become what everyone fears—another Hollow King, drunk on power and willing to destroy everything."

"Good. Fear like that keeps you honest." Lysse gripped her shoulder briefly. "The first Hollow King's mistake wasn't wanting power. It was forgetting why he wanted it in the first place. Hold onto your reasons, girl. They'll be your lifeline when everything else falls apart."

The voyage to the Trench took four days of hard sailing. The weather held, but Kerra could feel the tide-veins growing weaker as they traveled south. By the third day, even Lysse's wind-weaving was noticeably less effective, her ability to call favorable breezes diminished to the point where they had to rely more on oars than sails.

"It's worse than I thought," Lysse admitted to Davin and Kerra in the captain's cabin. "At this rate of decay, the entire network will collapse within weeks. Maybe days."

"Which means the Hollowed are close to completing their work," Davin said. "We need to hurry."

Kerra spent the journey training, working with Davin to refine her combat techniques and build her endurance. She also practiced sensing at extreme distances, reaching out ahead of the ship to feel for void-weavings and other signs of Hollowed activity.

On the fourth day, she found something.

"There," she said, pointing to a stretch of ocean that looked no different from the miles they'd already crossed. "I feel a massive concentration of void-weaving. Multiple layers, incredibly complex. It's like a... a funnel? Collecting energy from all the damaged veins and channeling it downward."

"Downward to what?" Lysse asked.

"The Rift. It has to be." Kerra closed her eyes and pushed her awareness deeper, fighting through the discomfort of sensing at such range. "I can feel something huge down there. Ancient and cold and so, so hungry. And it's aware of us. It knows we're coming."

"The Hollow King," Davin breathed. "We're close."

Lysse immediately altered course, steering the Stormrunner toward the coordinates Kerra indicated. As they drew closer, the ocean itself seemed to change. The water grew darker, almost black, and unnaturally still. No fish, no birds, no signs of life at all. Just an oppressive silence that made everyone aboard speak in whispers.

"The Deadwater," one of the crew muttered. "Heard stories about it. Ships that sail these waters don't come back."

"Cheerful thought," Davin said dryly. But his hand rested on the hilt of his belt knife, and his stone-weaver's instincts were clearly screaming warnings.

They sailed for another hour before Kerra felt it—a sudden surge of power from below, followed by a change in the Hollow King's presence. He'd been passive before, just a vast sleeping presence. Now he was active, aware, reaching upward through the water with tendrils of void-tide that made Kerra's skin crawl.

"Something's happening," she warned. "He's—"

The water erupted.

Not with a pillar this time, but with something far worse. A ship rose from the depths, water streaming from its hull in sheets. It was ancient, its timbers black with age and pressure, its sails shredded into spectral fragments. And standing on its deck were figures in robes the color of deep water shadows.

The Hollowed. At least a dozen of them, all clearly powerful weavers based on the resonances Kerra could feel. And leading them, standing at the raised aft deck, was a figure who radiated such concentrated void-tide power that Kerra could barely stand to look at them.

"Surrender the resonant," a voice called across the water, amplified by wind-weaving. "Give us the girl, and you may leave here alive."

"Not likely," Lysse called back. "How about you all stand down and answer for your crimes instead?"

The lead figure laughed. "Crimes? We serve the true master of these tides. When he rises, your petty laws and borders will mean nothing. The division ends. The unity returns. And those who stand with us will be exalted."

"And those who don't?" Davin asked.

"Will provide fuel for his hunger." The figure raised a hand, and Kerra felt void-tide weavings spring to life all around them. "Last chance. The girl, or everyone aboard your ship dies."

Kerra looked at Lysse and Davin, saw the same grim determination in both their faces. They weren't going to surrender her. Which meant they were about to fight for their lives against a force that massively outmatched them.

"Well," Kerra said quietly. "I guess it's time to find out if I'm as strong as Davin thinks I am."

She stepped to the Stormrunner's rail, faced the Hollowed's vessel, and let her barriers drop completely. All five tides roared through her at once—water and wind and stone and flame and the vast empty spaces between them. She was a resonant, possibly the most powerful weaver in generations.

Time to prove it.

"You want me?" she shouted across the water. "Come and take me."

And the battle for Vaetheris' future began.

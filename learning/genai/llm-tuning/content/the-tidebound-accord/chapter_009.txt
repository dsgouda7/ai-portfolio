Chapter 9: The Warning

Kerra woke to pain. Every nerve in her body felt like it had been scoured with salt and fire, and her head throbbed with a rhythm that matched her heartbeat. She tried to sit up and immediately regretted it, falling back against the bunk with a groan.

"Easy." Davin's voice came from somewhere to her left. "You channeled enough power to kill most weavers ten times over. The fact that you're alive at all is either miraculous or proof that resonants are tougher than normal humans. Possibly both."

Kerra managed to turn her head and found Davin sitting in a chair beside her bunk, looking almost as exhausted as she felt. His face had new lines etched into it, and his hair seemed to have gone whiter overnight.

"How long was I out?"

"Two days. We're almost back to Deepwater Crossing, though I'm not sure what we'll find when we arrive. The Hollow King's followers have had two days to prepare for his full release, and without the seal holding him, he's only getting stronger."

Memory rushed back—the battle, the temporary binding, the Hollow King's mocking smile as they fled. "Did he follow us?"

"No. Lysse's been checking constantly, but there's no sign of pursuit. He seems content to let us deliver our warning. Probably thinks it won't matter, that he's already won."

"Has he?"

Davin was quiet for a long moment. "I don't know, Kerra. He broke a seal that five resonants created together. We barely managed to slow him down, and that nearly killed all of us. I don't know how we're supposed to stop him permanently."

Kerra forced herself to sit up despite the pain, needing to see his face clearly. "Davin, tell me the truth. Is there any way to win this? Or are we just buying time before the inevitable?"

"There's always a way," Davin said, but he didn't sound convinced. "We just haven't found it yet. But running, hiding, giving up—those definitely lead to failure. So we keep fighting. Keep looking for options. Keep hoping that somewhere in all the old knowledge or new possibilities, there's an answer we haven't considered."

It wasn't the reassurance Kerra had hoped for, but it was honest. She appreciated that.

She spent the next hours slowly recovering her strength, drinking broth and forcing herself to eat despite her protesting stomach. The other weavers who'd formed the binding were in various states of recovery. Sera had regained consciousness but couldn't summon even a spark of water-tide—her well was completely dry and would take weeks to refill. Mikel could barely move his hands, the nerve damage from channeling so much flame requiring careful treatment. Lysse was on her feet but hollow-eyed, pushing herself through sheer will to keep the ship running.

Only Davin seemed relatively whole, though Kerra suspected that was deceptive. Stone-weavers were good at enduring, at hiding their damage beneath stoic exteriors. He was probably hurting as much as anyone.

On the third day, they sighted Deepwater Crossing. Or rather, what remained of it.

Kerra stood at the rail beside Lysse and stared in horror at the devastation. Half the floating platforms had been destroyed, sunk or burned or simply torn apart. The crystal-clear sections of floor that had allowed views into the Deepwater Enclaves below were shattered, and she could see bodies floating in the water.

"What happened?" Kerra whispered.

"War," Lysse said flatly. "Someone—probably Hollowed agents—convinced both sides that the other launched a first strike. While we were fighting the Hollow King, the Enclaves and Holds were tearing each other apart."

They navigated carefully through the wreckage, looking for survivors. They found them eventually—clusters of people on the remaining intact platforms, armed and suspicious, treating anyone who approached as potential enemies.

It took Lysse's reputation as a former Holds officer and her obvious battle-damage to convince anyone to listen. Finally, they were allowed to dock and escorted to an emergency council chamber where representatives from both Enclaves and Holds had gathered under an uneasy truce.

Kerra recognized none of them, these survivors of political machinations that had killed the previous leadership. They looked tired, angry, and desperate for someone to blame.

"You claim the Hollow King has risen," one of the Enclave representatives said after they'd delivered their report. Her name was Coral Master Tethys, and her water-adapted features were drawn with stress. "You claim an ancient evil is responsible for the tide-vein sabotage, not our political enemies. You'll forgive me if that sounds remarkably convenient."

"It's the truth," Kerra said. "I faced him myself. He's real, he's powerful, and he's coming."

"And who are you, exactly?" This from a Holds general named Korvin, whose wind-weaver marks stood out starkly on his weathered face. "An unbound girl who claims to be a resonant? How do we know you're not Hollowed yourself, trying to sow more chaos?"

"Because if I were Hollowed, I'd have let you keep fighting each other," Kerra shot back. "The Hollow King wants division and weakness. He wants you at each other's throats instead of united against him."

"Or that's what you want us to believe while his real plan unfolds elsewhere." Korvin leaned forward aggressively. "I fought in the wars twenty years ago, girl. I know how disinformation works. You present a greater threat to unite the enemy against while your real objectives proceed quietly."

"This isn't a military strategy," Davin interjected. "This is the literal end of the world if we don't act together."

"The end of the world. How dramatic." Tethys's expression was skeptical. "And what exactly do you propose we do about this supposed ancient evil? Even assuming he's real and as powerful as you claim, what could we possibly do to stop him?"

"We need to create a new seal," Kerra said. "Stronger than the first one, drawing on weavers from every faction, every tradition. If we can gather enough skilled weavers and coordinate their efforts—"

"You mean place our most powerful weavers under your control," Korvin interrupted. "Let you channel all that power through yourself. That's what resonants do, isn't it? Use others as fuel for their own ambitions?"

Kerra felt her temper flare. "I nearly died creating a temporary binding that bought us time to warn you! I'm not trying to use anyone. I'm trying to save everyone."

"So you say. But history teaches us that would-be saviors often become tyrants given enough power."

"And history also teaches us that divided people fall," Lysse cut in sharply. "While you sit here debating whether to trust us, the Hollow King grows stronger. His followers are still out there, still working to destabilize whatever remains of social order. If you don't act soon, there won't be anyone left to save."

The council chamber erupted into argument. Enclave representatives insisted that any response had to prioritize protecting the water-weavers who formed the backbone of their society. Holds generals demanded that wind-weavers maintain control of any military response. Everyone seemed more concerned with their faction's interests than the collective threat.

Kerra listened to them argue for ten minutes before she couldn't take it anymore. She stood, letting her frustration translate into power, and every tide-garden plant in the chamber—brought in to provide light and demonstrate the restored tides—began to glow brightly.

"Enough!" Her voice carried wind-weaving amplification, making everyone wince. "You want to argue about who's in charge? Fine. The Hollow King is in charge. He wins because you're too busy fighting each other to see the real threat. Is that really the legacy you want? To be remembered as the generation that let the world end because you couldn't put aside your petty conflicts?"

The chamber fell silent. Every eye fixed on Kerra, some fearful, some angry, most just exhausted.

"You're right to be suspicious," she said more quietly, letting the plant-glow fade. "I'm young, untested, and I'm asking you to trust me with enormous power. I can't give you proof that I'm trustworthy because trust doesn't work that way. All I can do is tell you what I know: the Hollow King is real, he's coming, and if we don't stand together, we all die. Separately, painfully, and for nothing."

Tethys and Korvin exchanged glances. Some unspoken communication passed between them.

"Show us," Tethys said finally. "Show us your power. Prove you're actually a resonant and not just some talented weaver with delusions."

It was a reasonable request, but Kerra felt her stomach clench. Demonstrating her full resonant nature meant revealing exactly how dangerous she could be. It also meant opening herself to the tides completely, which after her recent exhaustion carried significant risk.

But they needed to see. Needed to understand the stakes.

"All right," Kerra said. "But you might want to step back."

She centered herself, breathing deeply, letting her mental barriers dissolve. The tides rushed in—all five of them, singing their disparate songs that somehow harmonized in her awareness. Water and wind and stone and flame and the empty spaces between them.

She raised one hand and called water from the air, forming it into a sphere that hovered above her palm. With her other hand, she summoned wind to make the sphere spin, faster and faster until it became a miniature cyclone. Then she added fire, not to consume but to illuminate, creating a glowing vortex of steam and light. Stone-tide she used to anchor the whole thing, giving it structure and stability. And void-tide she wove throughout, maintaining the boundaries between elements even as they mixed and merged.

The result was beautiful—a miniature storm contained in a sphere no larger than her head, all five tides working in perfect balance. She held it for ten seconds, twenty, thirty, until she was certain everyone had understood what they were seeing. Then she carefully unwove it, returning each element to its natural state.

The council chamber was utterly silent.

"Ancestors preserve us," Tethys breathed. "You really are one."

"Now do you believe me?" Kerra asked.

Korvin nodded slowly. "About you being a resonant, yes. About whether we should trust you..." He paused. "That remains to be seen. But I'll admit that if even half of what you've said is true, we need to set aside our differences and prepare for something unprecedented."

It wasn't full acceptance, but it was a start. Over the next hour, they hammered out the basics of a response plan. Messengers would be sent to every settlement in the archipelago, warning of the threat and calling for skilled weavers to gather at Deepwater Crossing. Defenses would be prepared, though no one was quite sure what kind of defenses could stop a being who could unmake reality itself. And Kerra would work with Davin and whatever weaving experts they could gather to refine the binding technique, looking for ways to make it stronger and more sustainable.

It was a long shot. Everyone in that chamber knew it. But it was better than surrendering without a fight.

As they left the council chamber, Lysse pulled Kerra aside. "That was well done. You made them see reason."

"I made them see what I am," Kerra corrected. "I'm not sure that's the same thing as trust."

"Trust is earned over time. Right now, we just need cooperation." Lysse's scarred face was thoughtful. "Though I'll admit, watching you wield all five tides like that... it was beautiful and terrifying in equal measure. I can see why the first resonants scared everyone."

"I scare myself sometimes," Kerra admitted. "Every time I use that much power, I feel the temptation to use more. To see what I could do if I really pushed my limits."

"Then don't push them. Use only what you need, when you need it, and remember why you're using it in the first place." Lysse gripped her shoulder briefly. "You're not the Hollow King, Kerra. You're nothing like him. He forgot his humanity. You're still fighting to preserve yours."

Kerra wanted to believe that. But she couldn't forget how good it had felt to demonstrate her power in that chamber, to see the fear and awe in everyone's eyes. How easy it would be to start demanding rather than asking, commanding rather than cooperating.

The razor's edge between savior and monster grew sharper with every step.

Chapter 7: Battle at the Deadwater

The Hollowed attacked first, their void-weaver leader hurling a lance of pure absence across the water. Kerra barely deflected it with a hasty stone-shield, the attack dissipating against hardened air with a sound like reality tearing.

Then chaos erupted on all sides.

Water-weavers among the Hollowed raised waves that shouldn't exist in the Deadwater's calm, sending walls of black liquid crashing toward the Stormrunner. Lysse countered with wind, her face straining as she fought against the weakened tide-veins, managing to deflect the worst of it. But spray still washed across the deck, and Kerra heard screams as some of it touched crew members and their skin began to blister.

"The water's corrupted," Davin shouted, raising his own stone-defenses. "Don't let it touch you!"

Kerra reached for the water-tide and tried to cleanse what she could, purging the corruption that the Hollowed had woven into it. It was like trying to strain poison from a river with her bare hands—painful, difficult, only partially effective. But enough crew members recovered their feet that they could continue fighting.

Flame arced from the Hollowed vessel, not natural fire but cold blue flames that burned without consuming. A flame-weaver among Lysse's crew countered with orthodox fire, and where the two met, there was a flash of canceling forces that left everyone temporarily blind.

Kerra sensed more than saw the stone-weaver attack coming next—not thrown projectiles but the ocean floor itself, rising up beneath the Stormrunner to capsize her. She dove into her own stone-weaving, reaching deep to meet the attack with opposite force, pushing down where the Hollowed pushed up.

For a moment, they were matched. Then Kerra pulled on reserves she didn't know she had, channeling not just stone but void-tide to reinforce her weaving, and the ocean floor subsided with a grinding rumble felt through every timber of the ship.

"Good," Lysse panted beside her. "But there are too many of them. We need a different approach than trying to match them weaver for weaver."

Kerra knew she was right. They were outnumbered at least two to one, and the Hollowed had the advantage of the corrupted Deadwater itself, which seemed to strengthen their dark weavings while weakening everything else.

Unless...

"I can turn that against them," Kerra said suddenly. "The corruption in the water—it's void-tide weaving, right? Boundaries made wrong, natural transitions twisted into something harmful."

"Yes, but—"

"I'm a resonant. I can perceive those boundaries, manipulate them. If I can cleanse enough of the Deadwater, I can cut them off from their power source."

Davin grabbed her arm. "That would require void-weaving on a massive scale. You'd be exposing yourself to exactly the kind of temptation that created the Hollow King."

"I know. But what choice do we have?"

Before either of them could respond, the Hollowed's leader struck again, and this time the attack was aimed directly at Kerra. She felt it coming—a spear of pure dissolution, meant to unmake her at the fundamental level. She deflected it, barely, and the force of the redirected attack carved a trench in the ocean's surface that took five seconds to fill.

"Impressive," the Hollowed leader called. "You have talent, girl. But talent without training will only get you killed. Surrender now and the master will teach you properly. You could be great."

"I could become a monster, you mean." Kerra gathered her power, feeling the tides align within her. "No thanks. I've seen what your 'master' offers, and I'm not interested."

She reached out with her void-weaving, not attacking the Hollowed directly but targeting the corrupted water around their vessel. The boundaries were wrong—she could feel it, see it with her resonant awareness. Water had been partially converted to something else, something that existed halfway between liquid and poison and raw void.

Kerra grasped those wrong boundaries and began to fix them.

It was agonizing work. Every correction required not just power but precision, finding exactly where water should end and void should begin, then forcing reality to conform to that proper structure. Her head throbbed, her vision blurred, and she could feel something dark and seductive whispering at the edges of her consciousness.

*Why fix it? Why not unmake it entirely? You have the power. You could dissolve their ship, their bodies, everything. It would be so easy...*

"Kerra!" Davin's shout cut through the whisper. "Stay with us!"

She shook her head, forcing focus. Fixing, not destroying. Healing, not harming. That was her anchor—the difference between guardian and monster.

The Deadwater around the Hollowed vessel began to clear, dark corruption bleeding away as Kerra restored proper boundaries. She felt the Hollowed's weavings weakening as their foundation crumbled, their power draining like water through cupped hands.

"Stop her!" the leader commanded, and suddenly all their attacks focused on Kerra.

Lysse and Davin threw themselves between her and the Hollowed, weaving defensive shields as fast as they could manage. The Stormrunner's crew joined in, even those without weaving ability using crossbows and harpoons to disrupt the Hollowed's concentration.

And Kerra kept working, sweat streaming down her face, blood trickling from her nose as she pushed herself beyond any safe limit. The corruption retreated, foot by foot, yard by yard, and the Deadwater began to return to normal ocean water—still dark, still deep, but no longer twisted into something that shouldn't exist.

"Enough!" The Hollowed leader made a sharp gesture, and their ship began to sink, dropping back beneath the waves with impossible speed. "You've won this round, little resonant. But the seal breaks tonight. When the master rises, we'll see how long your convictions survive."

They were gone in moments, vanished into the black depths. Kerra finally released her void-weaving and collapsed, every muscle trembling with exhaustion. Hands caught her—Davin and Lysse both—and lowered her carefully to the deck.

"Status," Lysse barked to her crew. "Casualties, damage, everything."

The reports came back grim. Two crew dead from the corrupted water, three more injured. The Stormrunner's hull was intact but her masts had taken damage from the flame-weaving. They had maybe half their original sailing capacity.

"Can we still reach the Rift in time?" Kerra asked weakly.

"If we row like our lives depend on it—which they do—then maybe. Maybe." Lysse looked down at her. "That was either the bravest or stupidest thing I've ever seen, cleansing that water. You came close to losing yourself."

"I know. I felt it. The temptation to just... let go. To use all the power at once and burn everything away." Kerra shuddered. "Now I understand how the first Hollow King fell."

"But you didn't fall," Davin said firmly. "You held your ground, did what needed doing, and stopped when the job was finished. That's what makes you different from him."

Kerra wanted to believe that. But she couldn't forget how good it had felt, for just a moment, to hold that much power. How easy it would have been to keep going, to purge not just corruption but everything that stood in her way.

She was walking a razor's edge between savior and destroyer. And the footing was getting shakier with every step.

They buried the dead at sea, wrapped in canvas and committed to the deep with words of honor and remembrance. Then they turned all effort toward reaching the Abyssal Rift before the Hollowed could complete their work.

Kerra recovered enough to return to her sensing duties, reaching ahead to track the void-weaving funnel she'd detected before. It was closer now, maybe only hours away at their current speed. And beneath it, she could feel the Hollow King's presence growing stronger with each passing minute.

"They're feeding him the stolen tide-energy," she reported. "Every damaged vein across the archipelago, all that power being funneled down to where he's sealed. He's using it to break his bonds from the inside while his followers weaken the seal from the outside."

"How long until he's free?" Lysse asked.

"Hours. Maybe less." Kerra met her eyes. "We're not going to get there in time to stop them from breaking the seal. We're going to have to face him."

"Face the Hollow King himself." Davin laughed, but there was an edge of hysteria to it. "Of course we are. Why would this be simple?"

"Can you fight him?" Lysse asked Kerra directly. "Can a resonant kill another resonant?"

"I don't know. Maybe. If I'm willing to use enough power." Kerra looked down at her hands, which still trembled slightly from her earlier exertions. "But using that much power is exactly how he became what he is. How do I fight him without becoming him?"

"You find another way," Davin said. "The first resonants didn't kill the Hollow King—they sealed him. Five weavers working together, creating boundaries and wards that could hold him. Maybe that's what we need to do."

"We don't have five resonants. We have one exhausted girl, a disgraced scholar, and a ship full of brave but ordinary sailors."

"Then we improvise." Lysse's scarred face was determined. "I'm a wind-weaver. Davin's stone. Mikel down in the hold is flame-blessed, and Sera's been water-weaving since she was six. That's four tides among us. With you able to touch all five..." She trailed off, thinking. "What if we work through you? Use you as a conduit, combining our strength into a single binding?"

"That's... not how cooperative weaving normally works," Davin said slowly. "But then, this entire situation is abnormal. It might be possible."

"It'll hurt," Kerra said with certainty. "Channeling that much power through a single person—even a resonant—would be like trying to force a river through a reed."

"As opposed to facing down an ancient evil bent on unmaking reality, which I'm sure will be perfectly comfortable," Lysse shot back. "We work with what we have, Kerra. And what we have is you."

Kerra wanted to argue, but she couldn't. This was why she'd trained, why she'd left the safety of the Windskimmer, why she'd accepted her nature as a resonant instead of running from it. Because when the moment came, someone had to stand between the world and destruction.

She just wished that someone didn't have to be her.

"All right," she said quietly. "Gather everyone who can weave. Davin, teach them the binding patterns from the old records. Lysse, get us to the Rift as fast as this ship can possibly move. And someone get me food, water, and every stimulant we have on board. I'm going to need all the strength I can muster."

As the crew mobilized, Kerra allowed herself one moment of doubt. One quiet acknowledgment of how terrified she was, how unprepared, how young and inexperienced for a task that had defeated legendary weavers.

Then she pushed the doubt aside and got to work. Terror was a luxury she couldn't afford. The Hollow King was rising, and ready or not, she was all that stood in his way.

The sun set on a world that might not see another dawn. And in the deepening darkness, the Stormrunner raced toward the Abyssal Rift, carrying the last desperate hope of a people who didn't even know they were about to die.

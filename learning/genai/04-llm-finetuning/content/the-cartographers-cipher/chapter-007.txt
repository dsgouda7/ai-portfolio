Chapter 7: The Council Chamber

The city council chamber was packed beyond capacity. Reporters lined the walls, cameras positioned at every angle. Citizens filled every seat and crowded into the aisles despite fire code regulations. The exposure of the founding families' fraud had electrified the city, and everyone wanted to witness the reckoning.

Elena sat in the front row, flanked by Detective Chen and Captain Martinez. Across the aisle sat Dr. Rebecca Kahale and her siblings, their lawyer beside them. Marcus Blackwell sat several rows back, his expression carefully neutral. Sophia Winters was under police protection at a safe house, her testimony recorded and ready to be presented.

The council members filed in, looking overwhelmed by the attention and the gravity of what they were about to discuss. Mayor Patricia Granger, a career politician known for avoiding controversy, called the meeting to order with visible reluctance.

"We are here today to address allegations concerning the city's founding and subsequent property transfers. These allegations have been made public, and the council must determine an appropriate response. I will now hear from the police department regarding their ongoing investigation."

Captain Martinez stood and approached the podium. "Thank you, Madam Mayor. The police department has confirmed that Adelaide Thorne was murdered to prevent her from revealing evidence of fraud committed during Ashmont Bay's incorporation in eighteen seventy-nine. We have recovered a map created by city founder Thomas Ashmont that documents falsified property surveys and illegal land acquisition. This map has been authenticated by multiple experts and is now part of our criminal investigation."

A murmur ran through the crowd. Martinez continued.

"We have also identified several suspects in Mrs. Thorne's murder and the subsequent attacks on investigators. Two individuals have been taken into custody from the assault team that attacked police officers three nights ago. Both are cooperating with our investigation and have provided testimony about who hired them."

Elena felt the tension in the room spike. This was the moment. The revelation of who was behind the conspiracy.

"The individuals in custody have testified that they were hired through an intermediary by Thomas Crane, an attorney representing Northern Trust Holdings. Mr. Crane, who is present in this chamber today, has been identified as a descendant of J. Mordecai, one of the original founding conspirators who was murdered in eighteen eighty-eight."

Every eye in the room turned to a man sitting near the back. Thomas Crane was in his fifties, distinguished looking, dressed in an expensive suit. He met the stares calmly, showing no emotion.

Martinez wasn't finished. "Mr. Crane has been working in partnership with Victoria Ashmont, current director of the Ashmont Trust, to consolidate ownership of properties fraudulently acquired during the city's founding. Their plan was to expose the historical fraud while maintaining control of the disputed properties, allowing them to profit from both the revelation and the subsequent legal settlements."

More gasps from the crowd. Mayor Granger looked shocked.

"Where is Victoria Ashmont?" she asked. "Is she in custody?"

"Mrs. Ashmont remains missing. However, based on evidence we've gathered, including financial records and witness testimony, we believe she orchestrated her own disappearance to avoid prosecution. A warrant has been issued for her arrest on charges of conspiracy to commit murder, obstruction of justice, and fraud."

Thomas Crane stood slowly. "Madam Mayor, I request permission to address these allegations."

Mayor Granger hesitated, then nodded. "You may speak, Mr. Crane. But I should warn you that anything you say can be used against you in criminal proceedings."

Crane approached the podium, moving with the confidence of someone who had planned for this moment. "Thank you, Madam Mayor. I want to acknowledge that much of what Captain Martinez said is accurate. I am indeed a descendant of J. Mordecai. My great-great-grandfather was murdered by the founding families of this city when he threatened to expose their fraud. His wealth was stolen, his name was erased from history, and his family was driven into hiding."

He paused, letting the words sink in. "I have spent the past fifteen years researching what happened, uncovering the truth, and planning justice for my ancestor. Not revenge, despite what the police suggest, but justice."

"Justice that included hiring killers?" Chen called out from his seat.

Crane turned to face him. "I hired no killers, Detective. I hired security consultants to protect my interests and to gather evidence. If those consultants exceeded their authority and committed violence, they did so without my knowledge or consent."

"That's not what they say."

"Then they're lying to save themselves. I am a lawyer, Detective. I know the law. I would never sanction murder or assault on police officers. That would undermine everything I've worked to achieve."

Mayor Granger intervened. "Mr. Crane, you admit to working with Victoria Ashmont to consolidate ownership of the disputed properties. How is that justice rather than profit?"

"Because, Madam Mayor, my plan was never to keep those properties. My plan was to acquire them, prove the fraud, and then transfer ownership to the rightful heirs, the Kahale family, who were the original victims. Victoria Ashmont was supposed to be my partner in this endeavor. Instead, she betrayed me and attempted to keep the properties for herself."

Dr. Kahale stood. "If that's true, where is the documentation? Where is proof of your claimed intention to transfer the properties to my family?"

Crane pulled a folder from his briefcase. "Right here. A trust agreement, signed three months ago, stipulating that all properties acquired by Northern Trust Holdings would be transferred to the Kahale family upon proof of the founding fraud. The trust is irrevocable and legally binding."

He handed the folder to the mayor, who examined it with her legal counsel. After a brief consultation, she looked up with a troubled expression.

"This document appears to be legitimate. Pending verification, it supports Mr. Crane's claim that he intended to restore the properties to the Kahale family."

The crowd erupted in confused conversation. Elena felt her understanding of the case shift once again. If Crane was telling the truth, if he really had planned to give the properties back, then he wasn't the villain. Victoria was.

But that raised a new question: if Crane hadn't ordered the killings, who had?

Elena stood and approached the podium. "Mr. Crane, if you didn't hire the assault team, who did? Someone murdered Adelaide Thorne and attacked multiple people. If it wasn't you, and it wasn't Victoria, then who?"

Crane's expression darkened. "That's what I've been trying to determine. When Adelaide was killed, I knew immediately that Victoria had acted without consulting me. I confronted her, demanded to know why she had escalated to violence. She denied involvement, claimed someone else was protecting the conspiracy."

"Who?"

"She wouldn't say. Or couldn't say. I'm not sure which." Crane pulled out another document. "But I have a theory. Look at this property deed from eighteen eighty-five. It transfers a large waterfront parcel from the Ashmont Trust to an entity called Guardians Limited. I've traced Guardians Limited through multiple corporate iterations over the past hundred and forty years. It still exists today as a private holding company. And it owns some of the most valuable property in the city."

Elena took the document, her mind racing. "Who owns Guardians Limited?"

"That's the question. The corporate structure is deliberately opaque, designed to hide the true ownership. But I've spent years investigating it, and I've discovered something interesting. The board of directors of Guardians Limited has always consisted of exactly five people, each representing one of the founding families. The Ashmont representative, the Blackwell representative, the Winters representative, the Kahale representative, and one additional representative whose identity is never disclosed."

"The Mordecai family," Elena said. "The fifth founding family that was supposedly extinct."

"Exactly. But they're not extinct. Someone has been sitting on that board, representing Mordecai interests, for over a century. Someone who has remained hidden, protecting the conspiracy, maintaining the fraud. And when Adelaide threatened to expose everything, that someone ordered her death."

Marcus Blackwell stood, his face pale. "Are you suggesting someone from my family is part of this hidden board?"

"I'm saying someone from each of our families is part of it. Including mine." Crane turned to face the crowd. "My great-grandfather, J. Mordecai's son, made a deal with the other founding families in the eighteen nineties. Instead of seeking revenge for his father's murder, he agreed to join the conspiracy, to sit on the Guardians board and share in the profits. The murder was covered up, the Mordecai name was erased, but the family continued to benefit from the fraud."

"Then you've been profiting from the conspiracy all along," Dr. Kahale said, her voice hard with anger. "While my family fought for justice, while we lost everything, you and the others continued to steal from us."

"Yes," Crane said simply. "And I can't undo that history. I can't give back what was taken from your family over generations. But I can try to make things right now. That's why I've been working to consolidate the properties, to gather proof, to set up the trust that would transfer everything back to you."

"But Victoria sabotaged your plan," Elena said.

"Victoria, or someone on the Guardians board. Someone who decided that exposure was too risky, that Adelaide had to be silenced, that anyone who got close to the truth had to be eliminated."

Captain Martinez stepped forward. "Mr. Crane, you need to tell us who sits on the Guardians Limited board. We need to identify and question all of them."

"I can give you four of the five names. The fifth, the current Mordecai representative, I don't know. My grandfather died without telling me. The identity is passed from generation to generation in secret."

"Give us the four you know."

Crane pulled out yet another document. "The Ashmont representative is Victoria's cousin, James Ashmont. The Blackwell representative is Marcus's uncle, Richard Blackwell. The Winters representative is Sophia's father, who is elderly and in poor health. And the Kahale representative is Dr. Kahale's uncle, Samuel Kahale."

Dr. Kahale looked shocked. "My uncle? That's impossible. He's spent his entire life fighting for our family's rights."

"Has he? Or has he been managing the fight, making sure it never succeeded, keeping your family focused on small battles while the real wealth remained hidden?"

The implications were staggering. The conspiracy hadn't ended with the founding generation. It had continued, passed down through families, maintained by a secret board that protected the fraud and profited from it.

And one of those board members, or perhaps more than one, had decided that murder was preferable to exposure.

Chen pulled out his phone, making calls. "We need to bring in all four named board members for questioning. And we need to find Victoria Ashmont immediately. She's either a victim of the conspiracy or a key conspirator, and either way, she knows who the fifth board member is."

Elena's mind was reeling. The case had gone from a simple murder to a historical fraud investigation to a multigenerational conspiracy involving multiple families and secret corporate structures. Every time she thought she understood the scope, it expanded further.

Mayor Granger gaveled for order, trying to control the chaotic crowd. "This meeting is adjourned pending further investigation by law enforcement. The council will reconvene when we have more information about the scope of the fraud and the identities of all involved parties."

As people filed out of the chamber, Elena approached Thomas Crane. "If you really want justice, if you really intended to make things right, help us find the fifth board member. Help us identify who ordered the killings."

Crane met her eyes. "I've been trying to do exactly that for months. Whoever they are, they're extremely careful, extremely well-hidden. But I have an idea."

"What?"

"Guardians Limited holds quarterly board meetings. The next one is tomorrow night. If we can surveil that meeting, we can identify all five board members, including the one who's remained hidden."

"Where is the meeting?"

"I don't know. The location changes each quarter, and only the board members are notified. But my contact in the organization, someone who's been helping me investigate from the inside, might be able to find out."

"Who's your contact?"

Crane hesitated. "Victoria Ashmont. She's been feeding me information for the past year, helping me build my case. If she's really disappeared, if she's really in danger, we need to find her before the board meeting. She's the only one who knows where it will be held."

Elena exchanged looks with Chen. Finding Victoria was now more critical than ever. Not just to solve Adelaide's murder, but to prevent more deaths and to expose the full scope of the conspiracy.

"We'll find her," Elena said. "And when we do, this ends. All of it. The fraud, the murders, the conspiracy. Everything comes out into the light."

Crane nodded. "That's what I've been working toward. That's what Adelaide died for. Let's make sure her death wasn't in vain."

As the chamber emptied, Elena stood with Chen, Sophia's documents, and Crane's revelations, trying to process everything. A secret board managing a century-old conspiracy. Multiple families complicit in ongoing fraud. Murder as a method of preserving secrets.

And somewhere in the city, Victoria Ashmont was either hiding or being hidden, holding the key to exposing it all.

Elena checked her phone and found a text from an unknown number, sent just minutes ago:

"You want to find Victoria? Look where the city began. Look where six became five. Look beneath the foundation. Midnight tonight. Come alone or don't come at all."

Elena showed the text to Chen. He swore under his breath. "Another trap."

"Or another chance to end this. The text mentions foundations. That's what Elijah Thorne's note mentioned. Maybe there's more hidden beneath the Founders' Memorial, something we missed."

"We already found the map. What else could be there?"

"I don't know. But whoever sent this text wants me to find out. And they chose midnight, after the board meeting would have concluded. That's significant."

"It's also incredibly dangerous. You're not going alone."

"I wouldn't dream of it. But we need to be smarter this time. More backup, better positioning, thermal imaging, drones, everything. We've been outmaneuvered twice. There won't be a third time."

Chen agreed, and they spent the afternoon planning. By evening, they had assembled a tactical response that would have made a military operation proud. Snipers on surrounding buildings, drones overhead, plainclothes officers scattered throughout the park, thermal cameras watching every approach.

If this was a trap, Elena would spring it on her terms.

If this was a genuine meeting, she would finally learn the truth about who had killed Adelaide Thorne and why.

Either way, by dawn, the case would be over.

One way or another.

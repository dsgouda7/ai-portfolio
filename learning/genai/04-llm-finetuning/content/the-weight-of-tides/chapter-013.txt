Chapter 13: Seven Days

Claire docked at dawn, exhausted and wired in equal measure. Sarah was waiting on the pier, arms crossed, expression hovering between relief and fury.

\"You went alone,\" Sarah said flatly. \"Against every principle of safe research practice and basic common sense, you went alone into deep water at midnight to meet unknown entities.\"\n\n\"I know. They shared information directly, downloaded it somehow into my memory. I can write it all down, but the photographs won't do it justice. Sarah, we need to bring others down. Show the town council, show skeptics, make it undeniable.\"\n\n\"In seven days?\"\n\n\"They said they'd cooperate. Allow observation, provide evidence. This is what we've been waiting for.\"\n\nThey hurried to the house, where Maya was just waking. Her face went through several emotions when she saw Claire's wet clothes and exhausted expression.\n\n\"You went without me,\" Maya said, hurt evident. \"We're supposed to be partners.\"\n\n\"I went because they insisted on alone. The descent into the deep-dweller city, the communication with Observer, the ecosystem maintenance the entities performed. And finally, the truth about Thomas Merritt's death.\n\nMaya was silent for a long time. \"They killed him.\"\n\n\"They influenced his existing condition at a moment when he was about to expose them prematurely. It's not the same as murder, but it's not innocent either.\"\n\n\"Yes it is. They chose his death over their exposure. That's murder, Mom. Premeditated, calculated murder.\"\n\nClaire couldn't argue. She'd been wrestling with the same conclusion, trying to reconcile the entities' sophistication with their willingness to end a human life for strategic purposes.\n\n\"What do we do with that information?\" Sarah asked. \"It undermines everything. If they killed Thomas to maintain secrecy, how can we trust them now?\"\n\n\"Because circumstances have changed. Secrecy is ending regardless. They've chosen to cooperate with managed disclosure rather than fight to maintain invisibility. And they told me truth when they could have hidden it. That has to count for something.\"\n\n\"Or they're manipulating you, using truth to build trust they'll exploit later.\"\n\n\"Maybe. But we're past the point of retreat. Seven days until the vote. We use whatever tools we have, including their cooperation, to prevent development. After that, we can address the moral complexity of coexisting with entities capable of calculated killing.\"\n\nThey spent the day organizing what Claire remembered. The city's location, its approximate population, the ecosystem services the deep-dwellers provided. The biological markers in water samples that could prove their existence. The acoustic frequencies used for communication.\n\nMore urgently, they planned observation missions. The entities had promised cooperation. Time to take them up on it.\n\nJake assembled a group: the town council, key community leaders, Angela Reyes, and a few carefully chosen skeptics whose conversion would matter. Twelve people total, the maximum the Anna Marie could safely carry.\n\n\"Are you sure about this?\" Jake asked. \"Bringing the council into direct contact? If it goes wrong, if they're frightened or hostile, we lose the vote immediately.\"\n\n\"If we don't show them, they'll vote for development based on economic anxiety and Seabrook's promises. This is our only chance to make the stakes real.\"\n\nThey set out at dusk, six days before the vote. Claire piloted while Sarah briefed the passengers on what they might see. Reactions ranged from eager curiosity to obvious skepticism.\n\nMayor Linda Harrison spoke for the skeptics: \"Dr. Merritt, I respect your research, but if this is some kind of hoax or misidentification, the political damage will be severe. Are you absolutely certain?\"\n\n\"I've been down there, Mayor. Communicated with them directly. This is as certain as observation can make anything.\"\n\nThey reached the coordinates as darkness fell. Claire dropped anchor and waited, hoping Observer would keep his word. Minutes passed. Then the now-familiar glow rose from the depths.\n\nGasps and exclamations from the passengers. Even the skeptics couldn't deny the lights, the geometric patterns, the obvious intelligence in their arrangement.\n\nA sphere surfaced, larger than the one Claire had entered. Observer's voice filled the air, now comprehensible to everyone:\n\n\"Greetings, surface-dwellers. We offer observation without threat. Those who wish may descend. Those who prefer may watch from above. Either way, you will see what your choices affect.\"\n\nSix people volunteered to descend, including Mayor Harrison and Richard Caldwell, who had forced his way onto the boat despite protests. They entered the sphere, which sealed and submerged.\n\nClaire watched their faces through the transparent walls as they descended past the underwater city. Wonder, fear, awe. This was real. Undeniably, overwhelmingly real.\n\nThe sphere rose thirty minutes later, carrying passengers who stumbled out in shocked silence. Mayor Harrison sat heavily on a bench, face pale.\n\n\"That's... An actual underwater civilization.\"\n\n\"And you want to build condos that would destroy their ecosystem,\" Claire said. \"Understanding the stakes now?\"\n\nCaldwell was quiet, face calculating. \"This changes nothing. Mr. Caldwell, I'm not sure Seabrook's development is in Willowport's best interests after all.\"\n\nCaldwell's expression darkened. \"Mayor, I'd reconsider that position carefully. Seabrook has significant resources and influence. Opposing us could have consequences for you personally and for the town.\"\n\n\"Is that a threat?\"\n\n\"It's a reality check. We're prepared to pursue this development with or without council approval. Legal challenges, eminent domain, federal override. We will build here. The only question is whether Willowport benefits from cooperating.\"\n\nJake stepped between them. \"Get off this boat, Caldwell. You've seen what's at stake and you still prioritize profit. That tells me everything I need to know about Seabrook's intentions.\"\n\nCaldwell left, but his threat hung in the air. They had enemies with resources, determination, and ruthlessness. Six days wasn't much time to counter that.\n\nBack at harbor, the group dispersed to process what they'd seen. Mayor Harrison pulled Claire aside.\n\n\"I believe you. I believe them. Make it national, international. The existence of non-human intelligence isn't a local issue. It's a species-level discovery.

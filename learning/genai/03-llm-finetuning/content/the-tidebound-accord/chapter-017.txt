Chapter 17: Shadows Return

The academy's fifth anniversary celebration was planned as a grand affair—demonstrations of cooperative weaving, exhibitions of student projects, speeches from dignitaries representing every major faction. Kerra had been drafted to give the keynote address, a responsibility she'd accepted with reluctance.

She spent the morning practicing her speech, trying to find words that captured the academy's mission without sounding preachy or self-congratulatory. It was harder than fighting ancient evils. At least with combat, the objectives were clear.

Davin found her in the academy library, surrounded by crumpled draft attempts.

"Stop overthinking," he advised. "Just speak from the heart. Tell them what the academy means to you personally. Everything else will follow."

"What if my heart is full of uncertainty and imposter syndrome?"

"Even better. Honesty about doubt is more compelling than false confidence." Davin sat across from her. "You've built something remarkable, Kerra. Twenty-three resonants found and trained, hundreds of regular students educated in cooperative techniques, two dozen successful community projects. You have every right to be proud."

Kerra wanted to accept the praise, but something nagged at her. "It feels too easy lately. Like we've solved the major problems and now it's just... maintenance."

"You're complaining about peace?"

"I'm suspicious of it. The Hollow King's followers didn't all die. Some escaped, scattered. We've been rebuilding for five years without any sign of them. Where did they go? What are they planning?"

"Maybe they realized their cause was lost and integrated back into normal society," Davin suggested. "Not everyone remains dedicated to failed movements."

It was possible, but Kerra's instincts disagreed. The Hollowed had been too organized, too committed. People like that didn't just give up.

The celebration began at midday, with the academy grounds crowded with visitors from across the archipelago. Kerra gave her speech—shorter and more personal than her drafts, heeding Davin's advice—to polite applause. Demonstrations followed, young resonants showing off their control while older students executed complex cooperative weavings that drew gasps from the audience.

It was going perfectly. Which is when Kerra's unease crystallized into certainty that something was wrong.

She slipped away from the main celebration, reaching out with her tide-sense to scan the academy grounds. Most signatures were familiar—students, instructors, guests she recognized. But there, at the edge of her awareness, several presences felt off. Not overtly hostile, but... muted somehow. Like they were hiding their true nature.

Kerra found Jarek and pulled him aside. "Do you feel it? The presences at the southern dock?"

Jarek closed his eyes, extending his own awareness. His face went pale. "Void-weavings. Someone's using void-tide to disguise their signatures. Just like the Hollowed did."

They found Senna and Toran, then quietly mobilized the academy's security protocols without alarming the celebration guests. Within minutes, they'd positioned themselves to intercept whoever was at the southern dock.

Four figures waited there, dressed in the robes of traveling scholars but radiating the distinctive signature of void-tide users. When they saw Kerra and her fellow resonants approaching, they didn't run. They simply stood and waited, as if they'd expected to be discovered.

"Kerra Valmont," the leader said. "We hoped to speak with you privately. Away from your students and the celebration crowd."

"Who are you?" Kerra demanded, water and wind already gathering at her call.

"Survivors. Of the Hollowed, though we no longer serve that name or that cause." The leader pulled back her hood, revealing a woman perhaps forty, with scars that suggested she'd been in serious combat. "My name is Thessa. These are my companions—all former followers of the Hollow King, all deeply ashamed of what we attempted."

"Give me one reason not to have you arrested right now."

"Because we have information you need. The Hollowed organization wasn't fully destroyed. A core group—maybe thirty individuals—escaped the post-battle purges. They've been in hiding, but they're planning something. Something that could undo everything you've built."

Kerra wanted to dismiss it as a lie, but her resonant-sense detected no deception. These people were genuinely frightened, genuinely trying to help.

"Why should I trust you?"

"You shouldn't," Thessa said simply. "We were part of a movement that nearly destroyed the world. Trust has to be earned. But we can offer intelligence about the surviving Hollowed leadership, their locations, their plans. In exchange, we ask for the chance to atone. To help prevent what we once facilitated."

Kerra looked at her fellow resonants. Jarek nodded slightly—he sensed sincerity too. Senna and Toran were more skeptical, but neither objected.

"All right," Kerra said finally. "We'll talk. But under controlled conditions. Any sign of betrayal and you won't get a second chance."

They convened in a secure room away from the celebration. Thessa and her companions explained what they knew. The surviving Hollowed leadership, led by a woman named Verden Ash, had retreated to a remote archipelago in the far south. They'd been rebuilding their organization, recruiting new members from among those disillusioned with the post-war reforms.

"They're not trying to raise another Hollow King," Thessa explained. "Verden's smart enough to know that's impossible now. But they are trying to destabilize the current order. They see the academy as the primary threat—you're proving that resonants can exist peacefully, which undermines their entire philosophy that power must dominate or be dominated."

"So they'll target us," Kerra said.

"Eventually. Right now, they're building strength. But Verden's patient. She'll wait until she has overwhelming advantage before making her move."

"How long?"

"Months, maybe a year. But she will come. And when she does, it won't be dramatic battle. It'll be subtle sabotage, assassination, campaigns to turn public opinion against resonants. She'll try to make you look like the threats she's always claimed you were."

It was a terrifying prospect. Kerra had spent five years building trust. Verden could destroy it in weeks with the right provocations.

"What do you suggest?" Davin asked, having joined the meeting.

"Preemptive action. Find Verden before she's ready to strike. Expose her plans, arrest her leadership, dismantle what's left of the Hollowed organization." Thessa met Kerra's eyes. "We can guide you. We know their hideouts, their security measures, their communication networks. But it'll mean leaving the academy vulnerable while you're gone."

"Or we could inform the authorities, let them handle it," Toran suggested.

"The authorities are infiltrated," Thessa said flatly. "Verden made sure of that. The official investigation will be compromised from the start. If you want this done right, you need to do it yourself."

Kerra felt the weight settling back onto her shoulders. She'd hoped for peace, for a chance to just teach and build. But apparently, the universe had other plans.

"I'll need to discuss this with the council," she said finally. "And with the other instructors. We can't just abandon our responsibilities here on the word of former enemies."

"Understood. But don't take too long. Every day you delay is another day Verden prepares." Thessa stood. "We'll remain in Deepwater Crossing, available if you decide to trust us. Just... consider that we're offering this information precisely because we don't want another catastrophe. We've seen what fanatical certainty produces. We don't want to live through that again."

After they left, the resonants gathered to discuss.

"It could be a trap," Toran said immediately. "Former Hollowed claiming they want to help? That's exactly what I'd do if I wanted to lure Kerra away from protected territory."

"Their sincerity felt genuine," Jarek countered. "I'd stake my sense on it."

"Senses can be deceived. Especially with void-weaving involved."

"So what do we do?" Senna asked. "Ignore the warning and hope it's false? That seems equally risky."

All eyes turned to Kerra. She was the senior resonant, the one who'd defeated the Hollow King, the academy's founder. Whether she wanted it or not, major decisions fell to her.

"We investigate," she said finally. "Carefully, with backup plans and contingencies. If Thessa is telling the truth, we need to act. If she's lying, we need to know that too. Either way, ignoring this isn't an option."

"I'll go with you," Jarek said immediately.

"As will I," Senna added.

Toran hesitated, then nodded reluctantly. "Fine. But I maintain this is probably a terrible idea."

"Most of my best ideas seem terrible at first," Kerra said with a slight smile. "It's kind of my pattern."

They spent the next week planning. The academy's other instructors would maintain operations while the resonants investigated. Davin would coordinate with the council to prepare official support if needed. Lysse, whose ship had arrived for the celebration, agreed to provide transportation.

"Thought you were done with life-threatening adventures," Lysse said as they prepared to depart.

"So did I," Kerra admitted. "Turns out the universe doesn't care about my preferences."

"The universe rarely does. But that's what makes it interesting."

They sailed south with Thessa and her companions as reluctant guides, heading toward confrontation with an enemy who'd learned from past mistakes and wouldn't make the same errors again.

Kerra stood at the Stormrunner's bow and watched the familiar waters give way to unknown seas. Five years of peace ending, possibly, with whatever waited at their destination.

But this time, she wasn't alone. She had skilled allies, clear objectives, and the experience of having survived one apocalypse already.

If Verden Ash thought she could destroy what they'd built, she was in for an unpleasant surprise.

Resonants were no longer frightened, scattered individuals easy to exploit or eliminate.

They were a community. And they protected their own.

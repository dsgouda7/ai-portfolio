Chapter 10: Gathering Storm

The next week was a blur of desperate preparation. Messengers sailed to every corner of the archipelago that could still be reached, calling for weavers to come to Deepwater Crossing. Some came willingly, convinced by reports of the Hollow King's rise. Others came reluctantly, forced by their respective leaderships. Many never came at all, either dead in the recent conflicts or too afraid to leave their homes.

Kerra spent her days working with Davin and a rotating group of weaving experts, trying to refine the binding technique into something that could hold permanently. Every night she fell into bed exhausted, and every morning she woke to the growing certainty that they were running out of time.

The Hollow King hadn't attacked yet. That was almost worse than if he had. His followers continued their sabotage, weakening tide-veins and sowing discord, but the King himself remained at the Abyssal Rift, seemingly content to let anticipation do his work for him.

"He's waiting for something," Davin said on the fifth day as they reviewed yet another failed binding configuration. "Building strength, maybe, or waiting for his followers to complete some final preparation. But he will come eventually, and we need to be ready."

Kerra looked at the charts spread before them, showing tide-vein networks and convergence points and theoretical binding structures. It all felt inadequate—like trying to stop an ocean with a fishing net.

"What if we can't create a seal strong enough?" she asked quietly. "What if everything we're doing is just delaying the inevitable?"

"Then we delay it as long as possible and hope that buys enough time for someone smarter than us to find a real solution." Davin met her eyes. "I never said I had all the answers, Kerra. I just said giving up wasn't an option. There's a difference."

Their conversation was interrupted by a commotion from the docks. Kerra followed Davin outside to find a ship arriving—or rather, limping into port, her sails in tatters and her hull showing signs of recent combat.

The Windskimmer.

Kerra's heart leapt as she recognized the vessel. She was running before she consciously decided to move, pushing through the crowd gathering at the docks. The ship tied up, and Captain Breslin emerged from below decks, looking older and more worn than when Kerra had last seen him.

"Captain!" Kerra called. "You're alive!"

Breslin's tired face broke into a smile when he saw her. "Valmont! Heard you've been keeping busy. Something about ancient evils and the end of the world?"

"Something like that. What happened to you?"

"Ran into Hollowed raiders three days out from our destination. They seemed very interested in where we'd picked up our navigator. Fought them off, but..." His expression darkened. "Lost good people. Including Maren."

The news hit Kerra like a physical blow. Maren, who'd warned her about the Hollow King. Who'd introduced her to Davin. Who'd been kind when Kerra was broken and lost.

"How?" she managed.

"She bought us time. Used stone-weaving to collapse part of their ship while we escaped. Went down with them." Breslin's voice was rough. "Last thing she said was to tell you to trust yourself. Said you'd know what she meant."

Kerra felt tears burning her eyes but forced them back. There would be time for grief later. If they survived. "We're gathering weavers," she said, "trying to create a binding seal to contain the Hollow King. Can you and your crew help with the logistics?"

"Whatever you need, Valmont. Maren died believing you could save us all. Least I can do is help you try."

More ships arrived over the following days. Some brought weavers, others refugees fleeing the spreading chaos. The Hollowed grew bolder, their attacks more frequent and devastating. Entire settlements vanished overnight, their populations either converted to the Hollow King's service or simply unmade.

And through it all, Kerra trained. Not just the binding techniques but combat applications, defensive weavings, every scrap of knowledge that might help her survive what was coming. She pushed herself past exhaustion, past pain, past every reasonable limit.

Because deep down, she knew the truth that everyone was avoiding: when the Hollow King finally came, the binding seal might not be enough. Someone would have to face him directly, resonant to resonant, power against power.

And that someone was her.

On the tenth day after returning to Deepwater Crossing, Kerra woke to an unnatural silence. The city's usual ambient noise—waves, wind, voices—had vanished, replaced by an oppressive stillness that made her skin crawl.

She dressed quickly and rushed to the observation deck where Davin and Lysse kept watch. They were already there, staring south toward the horizon.

"He's coming," Lysse said without preamble. "I can feel it in the wind. Or rather, the lack of wind. The air is terrified."

Kerra extended her awareness and immediately wished she hadn't. The tide-veins were in chaos, their normal flows disrupted by a massive presence moving through them like a shark through a school of fish. The Hollow King was traveling through the network itself, using the very foundation of Vaetheris' magic as his personal highway.

"How long?" she asked.

"Hours," Davin estimated. "Maybe less. We need to get everyone in position now."

The council was roused, the alarm raised. Weavers who'd been training and preparing for this moment gathered at designated stations around the city. Non-combatants were evacuated to what passed for safe positions, though everyone knew there was no real safety if the binding failed.

Kerra stood at the central platform with the strongest weavers they'd assembled—twenty in total, representing all five tides and dozens of different traditions. They would form a cooperative binding unlike any attempted before, channeling their power through Kerra to create a seal that could theoretically hold the Hollow King indefinitely.

Theoretically.

"Remember the pattern," Davin instructed, his voice carrying to all assembled. "Each of you is a point in the greater weaving. Trust Kerra to direct the flow, trust the pattern to distribute the load, and above all, don't break the connection prematurely. The binding needs time to set, like mortar in a wall. Break too soon and everything collapses."

Twenty nervous nods. These were skilled weavers, but none had experience with anything on this scale. Kerra could feel their fear, their doubt, their desperate hope that somehow this would work.

She wished she felt more confident.

The Hollow King arrived as the sun reached its zenith, appearing not gradually but simply stepping from nothing into reality at the city's southern edge. He looked the same as before—robed in void, eyes empty of light, presence vast enough to make everyone's survival instincts scream.

But there was something different about him now. More solid, more present, as if he'd been only partially manifested before and now existed fully in the physical world. The air around him shimmered with power, and where his feet touched the platform, the wood aged centuries in seconds.

"Kerra Valmont," his voice rolled across the city like thunder. "You kept your promise. You warned them, prepared them, gave them hope. It won't make any difference, but I respect the effort."

"It'll make all the difference," Kerra called back. "You're not fighting one resonant this time. You're fighting all of us."

"Am I?" The Hollow King's empty eyes swept across the assembled weavers. "I see frightened people doing what they're told, placing their faith in a girl barely out of childhood. This isn't unified resistance. This is desperation."

"Desperation can be powerful," Lysse said from her position in the weaving circle. "Desperate people have nothing left to lose."

"True. But they have everything left to unmake." The Hollow King raised one hand, and the platform beneath him began to dissolve, its wood and metal and stone breaking down into component elements that swirled around him like a cloak. "Last chance, Kerra. Join me. Let me teach you what you could become. Together, we could remake this world into something better."

Kerra felt the temptation. With his power added to hers, they really could reshape reality. End suffering, eliminate conflict, create a paradise limited only by imagination.

All it would cost was her humanity. Her choice. Her self.

"No," she said simply. "I'd rather be human and flawed than perfect and empty."

The Hollow King's expression didn't change, but Kerra felt his disappointment like a cold wind. "So be it. I offered partnership. You chose opposition. Now you'll learn why no one has successfully opposed me for centuries."

He attacked, and the battle for Vaetheris' future began in earnest.

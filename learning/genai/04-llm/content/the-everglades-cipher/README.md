# The Everglades Cipher

**Genre**: Noir-Historical Detective / Literary Fiction  
**Primary Influences**: James Hadley Chase, John Grisham, Carl Hiaasen, Dan Brown, Elizabeth Kostova, Ruskin Bond  
**Setting**: Miami (Little Havana, Coconut Grove, Biscayne Bay), Florida Keys, Everglades, Caribbean (Windward Passage)  
**Time Period**: Present day (2024) with historical interludes (1289-1315)

---

## Synopsis

Jake Malone is a Miami private investigator — former Miami-Dade homicide detective, current resident of a houseboat called the Reluctant Witness at Dinner Key marina — who is hired by Elena Vasquez-Cortez, a marine biologist at the University of Miami, to find her missing father.

Her father, Arturo Vasquez-Cortez, is a seventy-eight-year-old Portuguese-born antiquarian bookseller who has operated La Luz de los Libros in Little Havana since 1978. He has been missing for three days. The police are treating it as a voluntary absence.

It is not a voluntary absence.

Arturo has spent thirty-four years decoding the Codex Almeida, a fourteenth-century Portuguese manuscript created by a navigator named Diego de Almeida who worked for the Knights Templar. The Codex encodes, in a two-layer cipher system (a grille cipher and a modal musical cipher), the coordinates of a hidden archive of Templar documents sealed in a Caribbean sea cave in 1307-1313 — before Philip IV of France executed the Templar dissolution.

Someone has discovered what Arturo has been working on and wants the coordinates before the scholarly community can claim them. Victor Andrade, a Miami real estate developer, has had Arturo transported to a fish camp in the Everglades.

Jake finds Arturo. They voyage to the island. They find the cave. They find the archive — 212 documents, including four letters from Philip IV of France that document an eleven-year intelligence partnership with the Templar Order and explain the real motivations for the dissolution. The archive is the most significant medieval document discovery in the Western Hemisphere.

The personal story runs in parallel: Elena's discovery of her biological origins, her fifty-year-deferred conversation with Arturo about what he knew and when, the resolution of a relationship built on mutual protective withholding. The ecological subplot tracks Elena's research on the Florida reef's partial recovery. Nacho Reyes's thirty-year dive logs provide the evidence that locates the cave.

The novel ends where it begins: Arturo at his worktable in the back room of La Luz de los Libros, under the lamp, with a new manuscript problem. He turns the first page. He begins.

## Stylistic Notes

The novel is written at the intersection of six literary traditions:

**James Hadley Chase** — Jake's narrative voice: spare, functional, third-person limited, the
hardboiled register of observation-without-editorializing. Dialogue that means more than it says.

**John Grisham** — Institutional texture: property records, police procedures, archaeological
jurisdiction, the specific slow machinery of legal processes. The drama of systems.

**Carl Hiaasen** — Miami's specific human comedy: the Cuban-American community's generational
politics, the ecological anxiety that runs under every Florida story, the named real places.

**Dan Brown** — Structure: historical interlude alternating with present-day thriller. The coded
mystery that rewards patience. The race to the physical location.

**Elizabeth Kostova** — Historical immersion: the sensory weight of the fourteenth century
rendered from inside. Place as character, not backdrop.

**Ruskin Bond** — The dignity of small human moments: Arturo and Elena's kitchen conversation,
Nacho at four in the morning on his dock, the heron on the piling. Subplots with their own
inner lives.

## Themes

1. **Preservation** — what survives, and under what conditions; the practices of careful
   attention that allow things to persist
2. **Long-term patience** — the value of work done slowly, accurately, without guarantee
3. **Institutional power vs. historical truth** — what institutions destroy and what survives them
4. **Florida** — the city that development built, the landscape it cannot cross
5. **The archive** — documents as the memory of intentions; the conversation across centuries

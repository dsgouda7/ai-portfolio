Chapter 2: The Ashmont Trust

The Ashmont Trust occupied the top three floors of a glass and steel tower overlooking the bay. Elena arrived fifteen minutes early, used the extra time to study the building's security, and noted three cameras in the lobby alone, all functioning. Whatever had happened at the municipal archives, it wouldn't happen here.

The receptionist, immaculate in a gray suit, verified her appointment and directed her to the private elevator. "Mrs. Ashmont is expecting you. Twenty-third floor."

The elevator was the kind that accelerated fast enough to make your stomach drop. Elena watched the city shrink beneath her through the glass walls. From this height, Ashmont Bay looked orderly and prosperous, its grid of streets radiating from the waterfront like Thomas Ashmont himself had drawn them. Which, of course, he had.

Victoria Ashmont waited in a corner office that commanded views of both the bay and the mountains beyond. She was in her early sixties, silver-haired, dressed in navy blue that probably cost more than Elena's monthly rent. But her eyes were sharp and worried, and her handshake was firm.

"Thank you for coming, Miss Voss. Please, sit."

Elena took the offered chair. "Before we discuss terms, I need to know what Adelaide Thorne told you yesterday."

"Direct. I appreciate that." Victoria moved to her desk, unlocked a drawer, and pulled out a folder. "Adelaide called me at nine-thirty AM. She said she had been researching her family's papers for a book she was writing about the founding families. In the process, she discovered documents that suggested the city's incorporation involved significant fraud. Land theft, falsified surveys, bribery of federal officials. She said the Ashmont name appeared repeatedly in these documents, and she wanted to give me the courtesy of seeing them before she went public."

"Did she send you copies?"

"No. She said it was too sensitive for email or mail. She wanted to meet in person. I suggested lunch today, but she insisted on authenticating the documents first. She said she had hired an investigator with expertise in historical cartography and document analysis. You."

"Did she tell you anything specific about what she'd found?"

Victoria opened the folder. Inside were several photographs, printed on standard office paper. "She sent me these. She said they were just a small sample."

Elena examined the images. One showed the same survey map she'd photographed at the crime scene, with its discrepancy in the northern boundary. Another showed a property deed transferring fifty acres of waterfront land to the Ashmont Trust in eighteen eighty-three, with a notation that the land had been "reclaimed from public trust per prior agreement." A third showed a letter from Thomas Ashmont to Elijah Thorne, dated eighteen ninety-two.

"The matter we discussed must remain between our families in perpetuity. The wealth generated from the arrangement has benefited all parties, but exposure would destroy not just our reputations but the legal foundations of the city itself. I have secured the relevant documents in a location known only to my heir. Should circumstances require their revelation, the map will guide the way."

Elena read the letter twice. "The map will guide the way. He's talking about a specific map, probably not one in the public archives."

"That's what Adelaide thought as well. She believed Thomas Ashmont had created a map showing the true boundaries and ownership of the city's founding properties. A map that proved the fraud. And she believed that map still existed, hidden somewhere."

"Did she tell you where?"

"No. She said she had clues but needed to verify them. That's why she was at the archives." Victoria's voice tightened. "Four hours after our call, she was dead. Someone didn't want her to find that map."

Elena set down the photographs. "Mrs. Ashmont, I need to ask you directly. Did your family know about this fraud? Have the Ashmonts been concealing stolen wealth for a hundred and forty years?"

Victoria met her eyes without flinching. "I don't know. My father never mentioned anything like this. Neither did my grandfather, as far as I know. The Ashmont Trust was established in nineteen twenty-five by my great-grandfather, Thomas Ashmont Junior, using the family fortune. Where that fortune came from, I always assumed it was legitimate business success. But now..."

"Now you're not sure."

"Now I'm not sure of anything. And that terrifies me." Victoria stood and walked to the window. "Do you understand what it would mean if this is true? The Ashmont Trust controls hundreds of millions in assets. We own property throughout the city, much of it dating back to the original incorporation. If those properties were acquired through fraud, through theft, the legal challenges could destroy everything. Lawsuits, criminal investigations, the disgrace of my family's name. And beyond my personal concerns, Miss Voss, think about the city itself. How much of Ashmont Bay's legal foundation rests on those original property transfers? What happens if they're all invalidated?"

"Chaos," Elena said quietly. "Legal chaos that could take decades to resolve."

"Exactly. Which gives numerous parties a motive to keep this buried. Including me." Victoria turned back to face her. "I want you to understand that. I have every reason to want this secret to stay hidden. But I also have a conscience. If my family built its fortune on theft and fraud, I want to know. I want the truth, Miss Voss, whatever it costs me."

Elena studied the older woman, looking for deception and finding only genuine distress. "Alright. I'll need access to your family's private papers. Anything from Thomas Ashmont or his immediate heirs, especially from the eighteen seventies through eighteen nineties."

"I'll arrange it. Most of the family archives are stored in a climate-controlled vault in the building's sub-basement. You'll have full access."

"I'll also need to speak with the other founding families' descendants. The Blackwells, the Winters, the Kahales. If this was a conspiracy, they were all part of it."

"I can provide contact information for Marcus Blackwell and Dr. Rebecca Kahale. The Winters family, I'm less certain about. They've been out of the public eye for generations. And the Mordecai family effectively vanished by the early nineteen hundreds."

Elena made notes. "One more thing. Adelaide's murder was professional. Close range, small caliber, clean execution. That suggests either a hired killer or someone with training. Do you have any idea who might have that kind of connection?"

Victoria's expression darkened. "Marcus Blackwell has a reputation. He's been involved in several lawsuits over property development, and there have been rumors, nothing proven, about intimidation tactics. Threats. One competitor's construction site burned down under suspicious circumstances."

"But murder?"

"I don't know. Marcus is ruthless in business, but I've never heard of him being violent personally. Then again, if hundreds of millions were at stake..." She trailed off.

Elena stood. "I'll start with the family archives today. Can you have them made available?"

"I'll have my assistant escort you down. The archivist will provide whatever you need." Victoria walked her to the door, then paused. "Miss Voss, one more thing. Be careful. Adelaide was careful, and it didn't help her. Whoever did this is willing to kill to protect their secret. Please don't underestimate them."

The vault was three floors below ground level, accessed by a key card and biometric scanner. The archivist, a thin man named Peter Huang, greeted Elena with nervous courtesy.

"Mrs. Ashmont called down. You're to have access to the Thomas Ashmont papers, eighteen seventy through nineteen hundred. That's boxes one through fourteen in the climate room. I'll get you set up."

The climate room was kept at a constant sixty-five degrees and forty percent humidity, perfect for document preservation. The fourteen boxes were already laid out on a long table. Elena pulled on the white cotton gloves Peter provided and began her examination.

The first box contained business correspondence from eighteen seventy through eighteen seventy-five, before the city's incorporation. Thomas Ashmont had been busy during those years, surveying the region, negotiating land purchases, and corresponding with potential investors. Elena found references to all the founding families, though the Mordecai family was mentioned only obliquely, as "our primary benefactor" or "the eastern interests."

The second box contained the incorporation documents themselves, official and meticulous. Property boundaries, ownership transfers, city charter, bylaws, elected officials. Everything looked legitimate, but Elena knew that legitimacy could be manufactured.

It was in the fourth box, documents from eighteen seventy-nine to eighteen eighty, that she found the first crack in the facade. A letter from Thomas Ashmont to "J.M." dated June 1879.

"Your concerns about the Kahale lands are understood. I have prepared the alternative survey as discussed, which will allow the transfer to proceed without federal oversight. The notation of 'voluntary sale' will satisfy the bureaucrats, and our agreed compensation to Chief Kahale will ensure his cooperation. The excess acreage will be divided according to our prior arrangement."

Alternative survey. Voluntary sale that required 'cooperation.' Excess acreage divided among conspirators. Elena photographed the letter and continued reading.

Over the next several boxes, she found more evidence of the scheme. The founding families had conspired to purchase land from the Kahale family at far below its value, using falsified surveys to hide the true acreage. They had then registered portions of that land as "public trust" while secretly maintaining ownership through shell corporations and trusts. The federal officials who should have overseen the transaction had been bribed or deceived.

But it was a letter in box eleven, dated eighteen eighty-eight, that made Elena's breath catch.

"Dear Elijah, the Mordecai matter is resolved as we feared it must be. J.M. threatened to expose our arrangement unless given majority ownership of the northern parcels. Such blackmail could not be tolerated. I have arranged for his permanent removal from our concerns. The eastern syndicate he represented has been compensated for their loss and will maintain silence. His papers are secured in the location we discussed. The map remains safe."

Permanent removal. Elena read the letter three times, her hands shaking slightly despite the cotton gloves. Thomas Ashmont, the city's revered founder, had conspired to murder J. Mordecai when the financier threatened to expose the fraud.

She photographed every page, her mind racing. A murder in eighteen eighty-eight and a murder yesterday. Both to protect the same secret. And somewhere, a map that proved everything.

"Finding what you need?" Peter Huang appeared in the doorway, making Elena jump.

"Yes, thank you. This is exactly what I was looking for."

"Mrs. Ashmont said to provide anything you require. I'll be at my desk if you need assistance."

After he left, Elena continued through the boxes. She found references to the map in several places. Thomas Ashmont had created it as insurance, a record of the true property boundaries and ownership. But he had hidden it, telling only Elijah Thorne where it could be found.

The last letter in box fourteen was dated March eighteen ninety-seven, two months before Thomas Ashmont's death.

"Elijah, my health fails. The doctor gives me months at most. I have told my son nothing of our arrangement. He will inherit the wealth but not the burden of its origin. The map and its guardians remain secure. Should the truth ever need to be revealed, you and your heirs will know where to look. The foundation must hold, but the truth must not be lost entirely. God forgive us our sins."

The foundation must hold, but the truth must not be lost. Adelaide Thorne had been Elijah's great-great-granddaughter. Had the secret been passed down through the Thorne family? Had Adelaide finally decided to reveal it?

Elena finished photographing the documents and returned the boxes to their places. She had evidence of fraud, of stolen land, of conspiracy and murder. But she didn't have the map. And without the map, she couldn't prove the current status of those stolen properties, couldn't trace them to modern owners, couldn't build a case that would hold up in court or the press.

Adelaide had been looking for the map. That's why she was at the archives, going through both Thorne family papers and city records. She had been following clues from the letters, trying to decipher where Thomas Ashmont had hidden his insurance policy.

And she had gotten close enough that someone killed her for it.

Elena took the elevator back up to Victoria Ashmont's office. The older woman looked up from her desk with anxious eyes.

"Did you find anything?"

"Yes. And it's worse than you thought." Elena showed her the photographs on her phone. "Your great-great-grandfather and the other founding families conspired to steal land from the Kahale family using fraudulent surveys. They bribed federal officials, created false documents, and hid the true ownership of valuable properties. And when one of the conspirators, J. Mordecai, threatened to expose them, Thomas Ashmont had him murdered."

Victoria's face went pale. "Dear God."

"Thomas created a map showing the true boundaries and ownership. He hid it somewhere, telling only Elijah Thorne where it was. Adelaide must have been trying to find it when she was killed."

"Do you know where it is?"

"Not yet. But I know Adelaide was close. The letters mention 'the foundation' and 'guardians.' Those might be literal or figurative. I need to trace Adelaide's research, see what she was looking at in the archives, figure out what clues she was following."

Victoria stood, visibly shaken. "Miss Voss, what happens when you find this map? What happens to the city, to my family, to everything built on those stolen properties?"

"I don't know. But Adelaide died trying to reveal the truth. Someone is killing to keep it hidden. That tells me the corruption didn't end in eighteen eighty-eight. It's still active, still profitable, still worth murdering for. And that means it needs to be exposed."

"Even if it destroys innocent people who had nothing to do with the original crimes?"

Elena met her eyes. "Your family has benefited from those crimes for a hundred and forty years. My job is to find the truth. What you and the city do with it afterwards is up to you."

She left Victoria Ashmont sitting at her desk, staring out at the city her ancestor had built on lies and stolen land.

Outside, the day had turned clear and bright, the rain washed away. Elena walked down toward the waterfront, thinking about maps and murders, past and present. She needed to talk to the other descendants, particularly Dr. Rebecca Kahale, whose family had been the primary victims of the fraud.

But first, she needed to understand Adelaide's research methodology. She needed to think like a librarian and historian, to follow the trail of documents and clues that had led Adelaide from family papers to the municipal archives to her death.

Elena pulled out her phone and called Detective Chen.

"I need the complete inventory from the crime scene. Everything Adelaide was looking at, everything in those archive boxes."

"Already being processed. I'll send you the list this afternoon."

"I also need to know if Adelaide visited the archives before yesterday. Sign-in logs, requests for specific collections, anything that shows what she was researching."

"I can get that. What did you find at the Ashmont Trust?"

"Evidence that the city's founding was based on fraud, theft, and at least one murder. I'll send you copies of everything."

A pause. "Elena, this is bigger than a homicide investigation."

"I know. But it started with one murder and it's still driving murders today. We solve Adelaide's killing, we expose everything else."

"Or we get killed ourselves."

"That's always a possibility in this job, Marcus."

Another pause. "Be careful. And keep me informed. If this really does involve the founding families, the political pressure on the department is going to be intense. I need evidence I can use."

"You'll get it. One way or another."

Elena ended the call and stood at the waterfront, looking out at the bay. Boats moved across the water, ferries and cargo ships and pleasure craft. The city hummed with life and commerce, built on a foundation of lies.

Somewhere in this city, a map existed that proved everything. And somewhere in this city, someone was willing to kill to keep it hidden.

Elena intended to find both the map and the killer, even if it brought down the entire corrupt structure of Ashmont Bay's founding mythos.

The truth, she had learned long ago, was worth the cost.

Now she just had to survive long enough to reveal it.

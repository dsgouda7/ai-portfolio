Chapter 5: Shots in the Dark

Elena dropped flat, pulling Sophia down with her. The gunshot had come from the west, near where Officer Rodriguez had been stationed. A second shot followed, then a shout, cut off abruptly.

Chen had his weapon drawn, moving toward the sound. "Tran! Rodriguez! Report!"

No answer.

Elena grabbed the map, shoving it back into the brass tube and tucking it inside her jacket. She pulled her gun and motioned for Sophia to stay down behind the monument.

"This was a trap," Sophia said, her voice shaking. "Someone knew we'd be here."

"Or they followed us." Elena scanned the darkness. The park's antique lamps created pools of light surrounded by deep shadow. Anyone could be hiding in those shadows, watching, waiting.

Chen crouched beside them, his phone to his ear. "Officers down in Ashmont Park. Shots fired. Need immediate backup and medical." He ended the call and looked at Elena. "We need to get to Rodriguez and Tran."

"We need to get out of here. Whoever's shooting has already taken down two armed officers. We're outgunned."

"I'm not leaving my officers."

Another shot rang out, this one striking the monument six inches from Elena's head. Granite chips sprayed across her face. The shooter had night vision or knew the park well enough to shoot accurately in the dark.

"Move!" Chen grabbed Sophia's arm, pulling her toward a cluster of trees twenty feet away. Elena provided covering fire, shooting toward the muzzle flash she'd seen in the western tree line, then ran after them.

They made the trees as another shot tore through the space where Elena had been standing. Whoever this was, they were good. Professional. Just like Adelaide's killer.

"We're pinned down," Chen said. "Backup will take at least five minutes. We need to keep moving, use the terrain for cover."

"The east entrance is closest," Elena said. "Fifty yards through open lawn. We'd be exposed."

"Better than staying here to die."

Sophia was hyperventilating, her eyes wide with panic. Elena gripped her shoulder. "Stay calm. Stay low. Follow us and don't stop moving. Understand?"

Sophia nodded mutely.

They moved east, using trees and landscaping for cover, staying low. Elena kept expecting another shot, kept waiting for the bullet that would end everything. But the shooter seemed to be repositioning, giving them a brief window.

They made it halfway to the entrance when Elena saw a figure moving through the shadows ahead of them. Not the shooter from the west. Someone else, cutting off their escape route.

"We've got company," she whispered.

Chen saw it too. "Two shooters. We're being herded."

"Or caught in a crossfire." Elena assessed their options. North was open lawn, suicide. South led deeper into the park, away from help. East and west were blocked by shooters. "The maintenance building. Southwest corner. It's solid brick, lockable. We can fort up there until backup arrives."

They changed direction, moving as quickly as stealth allowed. Behind them, Elena heard sirens. The backup Chen had called for, still minutes away. Too far to help.

The maintenance building loomed ahead, a squat brick structure with small windows and a steel door. Elena tried the door. Locked.

"Stand back." Chen kicked the door near the lock. It took three kicks before the frame splintered and the door flew open.

They tumbled inside, Elena pulling the broken door closed behind them. Chen dragged a metal shelving unit across the entrance as a barricade. The building smelled of fertilizer and motor oil. Lawnmowers and equipment filled the space.

"How long until backup arrives?" Sophia asked.

"Two minutes, maybe three." Chen checked his phone. "I'm not getting signal. Too much brick."

Elena moved to a window, carefully looking out. She saw flashlights in the distance, converging on their position. Not just two shooters. Four, maybe five. A professional team, not a lone killer.

"This isn't about killing Adelaide anymore," she said. "This is about destroying the evidence and anyone who's seen it. They're cleaning up loose ends."

"Then we're all loose ends." Chen checked his weapon. "I've got twelve rounds left. You?"

"Fourteen."

"Against four or five shooters with night vision and professional training. The math isn't good."

The sound of footsteps outside. Voices, low and coordinated. The team was surrounding the building.

Elena pulled out her phone. No signal for her either. They were cut off, outnumbered, outgunned. The brass tube with the map pressed against her ribs, a reminder of why they were about to die.

Then she heard a different sound. Car engines, multiple vehicles, approaching fast. The sirens were louder now, very close.

Someone outside shouted an order. The footsteps retreated, moving away quickly. The team was withdrawing, not willing to risk a confrontation with police backup.

Thirty seconds later, the park was flooded with light from police vehicles. Officers swarmed the area, securing the perimeter. Chen pushed aside the barricade and opened the door.

"We're in here! Detective Chen, with two civilians. Officers down near the Founders' Memorial."

Paramedics rushed toward the monument while officers secured Chen, Elena, and Sophia. Within minutes, the park was a crime scene, cordoned off and crawling with investigators.

Elena learned that Rodriguez had been shot in the shoulder, Tran had been knocked unconscious but was stable. Both would survive. The shooters had vanished, leaving behind only shell casings and spent rounds.

Captain Sarah Martinez arrived twenty minutes later, her expression grim. She was Chen's commanding officer, a veteran detective who had risen through the ranks through skill and determination. She listened to Chen's report without interrupting, her eyes occasionally flicking to Elena.

"So let me understand this," Martinez said. "You brought a civilian and two officers to excavate a historical monument in the middle of the night, without a warrant, based on a theory about a hundred-year-old conspiracy. In the process, two officers were shot and you nearly got yourselves killed. Is that accurate?"

"That's accurate," Chen admitted. "But we found the map. We have evidence of fraud and murder involving the city's founding families. Evidence worth killing for, as tonight proved."

"Show me."

Elena pulled out the brass tube and carefully extracted the map. Martinez examined it under a flashlight, her expression unreadable.

"This proves the founding families conspired to steal land and falsify property records?"

"It proves they knowingly committed fraud. Combined with the other documents I've found in the Ashmont archives and Adelaide Thorne's research, it builds a case that the city's incorporation was based on theft and bribery. And the conspiracy included at least one murder, possibly more."

Martinez rolled up the map carefully. "This is evidence in a homicide investigation. It's now part of the chain of custody. I'll have it analyzed and authenticated. In the meantime, all three of you will give full statements about tonight's events."

"Captain, we need to identify the shooters," Chen said. "This was a professional team, well-armed and coordinated. Someone with significant resources sent them."

"I'm aware, Detective. I'm also aware that whoever did this has already demonstrated they're willing to kill police officers. This investigation just became exponentially more dangerous." Martinez looked at Elena. "Miss Voss, I understand you're a licensed private investigator and were working for Adelaide Thorne. I'm officially requesting that you cease your independent investigation and turn over all evidence you've collected to the department."

"With respect, Captain, I was hired to do a job. Adelaide died before I could complete it. I owe it to her to finish."

"Adelaide is dead. You nearly joined her tonight. How many more people need to die before you accept that this is beyond private investigation?"

Elena wanted to argue, but Martinez was right. Tonight had proven that this wasn't a case she could handle alone. The conspiracy was larger, more dangerous, and more actively defended than she had anticipated.

"I'll turn over my evidence," she said. "But I want to be kept informed of the investigation's progress. And I want protection for the potential victims. If the founding families' descendants are targets, they need to be warned."

"Already in progress. Officers are being dispatched to Victoria Ashmont, Marcus Blackwell, Dr. Rebecca Kahale, and Miss Winters here. Anyone connected to the founding families will be under protection until we identify the threat."

They spent the next two hours giving statements, going over every detail of the evening. By the time Elena was cleared to leave, it was nearly two AM. She was exhausted, pumped full of adrenaline that was starting to crash, and acutely aware of how close she had come to dying.

Chen walked her to her car. "You okay?"

"Define okay."

"Fair point." He leaned against her car, looking as tired as she felt. "Martinez is right, you know. This is bigger and more dangerous than either of us realized. Professional hitters, multiple shooters, willingness to attack police officers. That's not desperation. That's confidence backed by serious resources."

"Which means someone with money and power is behind this. One of the founding families' descendants, protecting their fortune."

"Or someone else entirely. Someone we haven't identified yet." Chen ran a hand through his hair. "Elena, I need you to be careful. These people have already killed Adelaide and tried to kill us. They won't stop until either they're caught or everyone who knows the truth is dead."

"I know. But I meant what I told Martinez. I'm finishing this case. Adelaide hired me to find the truth, and I found it. Now I'm going to make sure it sees the light of day, whatever it takes."

"Even if it gets you killed?"

"Hopefully it won't come to that."

Chen's expression was serious. "This isn't a joke. You're not invincible, Elena. Neither am I. If we're going to survive this, we need to be smart, not heroic."

Elena met his eyes, seeing genuine concern there. Despite their complicated history, despite the way things had ended between them, he still cared about her safety. The knowledge was both comforting and painful.

"I'll be smart," she promised. "But I won't back down. That's not who I am."

"I know. It's one of the things I always loved about you. And one of the things that scared me the most."

He pushed off from her car and walked away before she could respond. Elena sat in her car for a long moment, processing everything. The map hidden for over a century, the professional hit team, the escalating danger. And beneath it all, the complicated tangle of her relationship with Marcus Chen, never quite resolved, always hovering in the background.

She drove home through empty streets, her mind still racing. The map was now in police custody, being analyzed and authenticated. Once it was confirmed as genuine, the implications would be enormous. Legal challenges to property ownership throughout the city. Lawsuits seeking damages. Criminal investigations into whether any current residents of Ashmont Bay had knowingly benefited from fraud.

And somewhere in the city, the people who had sent tonight's hit team were regrouping, planning their next move. They had failed to kill Elena, Chen, and Sophia. They had failed to destroy the map. But they wouldn't give up. Too much was at stake.

Elena's apartment was on the third floor of a converted warehouse, similar to her office but residential. She checked the locks, the windows, the fire escape. Everything seemed secure, but she kept her gun within reach as she collapsed onto her bed.

Sleep wouldn't come. Every time she closed her eyes, she saw the muzzle flash in the darkness, felt the granite chips from the near-miss shot spraying across her face. She had been inches from death. If the shooter's aim had been slightly better, or if she had moved slightly slower, she would be in the morgue next to Adelaide.

At four AM, she gave up on sleep and made coffee. While it brewed, she went through all the photographs she had taken over the past two days. The letters from the Ashmont archives. The hidden note from Elijah Thorne. The map itself, though those photos would likely be subpoenaed by the police.

She organized everything into a timeline, trying to build a clear narrative of the conspiracy. Eighteen seventy-nine, the fraudulent land acquisition. Eighteen eighty through eighty-eight, the distribution of stolen properties among the founding families. Eighteen eighty-eight, J. Mordecai's murder when he tried to blackmail the others. Eighteen eighty-nine, the creation of the Founders' Memorial with the map hidden in its cornerstone.

And then a gap of nearly a hundred and forty years before Adelaide discovered the truth and was murdered for it.

What had changed? Why had the conspiracy stayed dormant for so long, only to suddenly become lethal again?

Elena thought about the properties involved. Northern waterfront land, taken from the Kahale family, falsely registered as public trust, secretly owned by the conspirators. In the modern day, that land would be worth hundreds of millions, maybe billions. Waterfront property was the most valuable real estate in the city.

Who owned it now? The founding families' descendants? The city itself, believing it to be public trust? Some combination?

That was the question that mattered. Find out who currently controlled those properties, and she would find out who had the motive to keep the fraud hidden. Find the current beneficiaries, and she would find Adelaide's killer.

Her phone rang, startling her. It was barely five AM. She checked the caller ID: Dr. Rebecca Kahale.

"Miss Voss, I'm sorry to call so early. The police just left. They told me what happened at the park last night. Are you alright?"

"Bruised and shaken, but alive. How are you holding up?"

"Shocked. Terrified. A police officer is posted outside my house. They said I might be a target, that the people who killed Adelaide might come after me next."

"It's possible. If your family files suit to reclaim the stolen properties, you'd be a threat to whoever currently owns them."

"I spoke with my brother and sister. We're consulting with lawyers. If the map proves the fraud, we intend to seek full restitution for what was stolen from our family. That could be hundreds of millions in damages and property transfers."

"That makes you very dangerous to some very wealthy people."

"I understand that. But we can't let this opportunity pass. For generations, my family has known we were cheated, but we couldn't prove it. Now we have proof. We have to act."

Elena understood the impulse, but she also understood the danger. "Dr. Kahale, please be careful. Stay with the police protection. Don't go anywhere alone. These people have already killed twice and attempted to kill several more. They won't hesitate to add you to the list."

"I'll be careful. But Miss Voss, I need to know. Who do you think is behind this? Which of the founding families hired those shooters?"

"I don't know yet. But I'm going to find out."

After ending the call, Elena sat with her coffee and thought about the question. Who had hired the hit team? Not Victoria Ashmont, who seemed genuinely committed to exposing the truth, even at cost to her family. Probably not Sophia Winters, who had nearly been killed alongside Elena and Chen. That left Marcus Blackwell as the most obvious suspect among the known descendants.

But there was another possibility. The Mordecai family, supposedly extinct, supposedly murdered and erased. What if they weren't extinct? What if a Mordecai heir existed somewhere, had recently discovered their heritage, and wanted revenge against the families that had murdered and cheated their ancestor?

That would explain the violence, the professional execution. Not a defensive move to protect stolen wealth, but an offensive move to punish the guilty.

Elena needed to research the Mordecai family more thoroughly. She needed to find out if any heirs existed and where they were now.

As the sun rose over Ashmont Bay, Elena showered, dressed in fresh clothes, and prepared for another day of investigation. She was exhausted, traumatized, and outmatched. But she was also close to the truth. Adelaide's murder, the hidden map, the conspiracy. All the pieces were there. She just had to put them together before someone put a bullet in her.

Her phone buzzed with a text from Detective Chen: "Map authenticated as genuine 1880s document. Forensics confirmed it's Thomas Ashmont's handwriting. Captain Martinez calling a press conference at noon to announce major break in founding families fraud case. Things are about to get very public."

Elena smiled grimly. Once the information went public, the pressure on the conspirators would intensify. They couldn't kill everyone who knew the truth, not once it was broadcast to the entire city. But they could try to kill the people most dangerous to them before the press conference.

She checked her gun, made sure her phone was fully charged, and headed out the door. Time to find out who was willing to kill to protect a hundred-and-forty-year-old secret.

And time to make sure they paid for Adelaide Thorne's murder.

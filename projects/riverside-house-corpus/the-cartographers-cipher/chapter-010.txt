Chapter 10: The Scholar

Anna Chen was younger than Elena expected, barely into her late twenties, wearing jeans and a university sweatshirt, her hair pulled back in a simple ponytail. She looked more like someone heading to a study group than someone claiming a forty-million-dollar inheritance.

"Miss Voss, it's an honor to meet you. I followed your investigation into the Ashmont conspiracy closely. Your work was brilliant."

"Thank you. Your cousin Marcus speaks highly of you."

Anna's eyes widened. "Marcus told you about me? I haven't talked to him in months. I've been so focused on my dissertation research that I've been terrible about keeping in touch with family."

"He mentioned you're studying Pacific Northwest history."

"Specifically, I'm researching Chinese American involvement in early Seattle and regional development. It's a history that's been largely ignored or minimized in mainstream accounts." Anna settled into her chair, more comfortable discussing academics than inheritance claims. "That's actually how I discovered my connection to the Mordecai family."

"Tell me about that."

Anna pulled out a laptop and opened a presentation. "My research focuses on Chinese merchants who came to the Pacific Northwest in the mid to late eighteen hundreds. One of these merchants was a man named Liu Wei, my great-great-great-grandfather. He ran a successful trading business in Seattle, dealing in silk, tea, and other Asian goods. He was prosperous and well-respected, at least within the Chinese community."

She clicked to the next slide, showing a faded photograph of an Asian man in Victorian-era clothing.

"In eighteen seventy-five, Liu Wei made a business trip to what would become Ashmont Bay. He was exploring potential sites for a trading post. While there, he met J. Mordecai, who was already planning the city's founding and looking for investors. They formed a partnership. Liu Wei provided capital and trade connections, Mordecai provided local knowledge and connections to American business interests."

"There's no record of Liu Wei being involved in Ashmont Bay's founding."

"Because Chinese involvement was deliberately erased from the historical record. Chinese Exclusion Act, anti-Asian sentiment, deliberate historical whitewashing. It happened throughout the Pacific Northwest. Chinese workers built the railroads but don't appear in commemorative photographs. Chinese merchants funded businesses but weren't listed as partners. Chinese families owned property but had to hold it through white proxies."

Elena leaned forward, intrigued despite her skepticism. "You're saying Liu Wei was a hidden investor in Ashmont Bay's founding?"

"More than that. He was J. Mordecai's primary financial backer. The 'eastern syndicate' that Mordecai supposedly represented? That was Liu Wei's merchant network. The capital Mordecai brought to the founding? That was largely Liu Wei's money."

"That's a substantial claim. What evidence supports it?"

Anna clicked through more slides, showing ledgers, contracts, letters. "Family business records, maintained in Chinese and therefore overlooked by English-speaking historians. Correspondence between Liu Wei and J. Mordecai discussing their partnership. Contracts specifying profit-sharing arrangements. It's all documented in our family papers."

"If Liu Wei provided the capital and was Mordecai's partner, why didn't he claim his share when Mordecai was murdered?"

"He tried. But when Mordecai was killed and the conspiracy covered it up, Liu Wei's claims were dismissed. He was Chinese, had no legal standing, couldn't prove the partnership in American courts that routinely excluded Asian testimony. The founding families simply denied any connection to him and kept the money. Liu Wei died broke in nineteen hundred, all his wealth stolen by the same conspiracy that murdered his partner."

Elena sat back, processing this. If Anna's claim was true, it added another dimension to the Ashmont conspiracy. Not just theft from indigenous people, but theft from Chinese investors as well. A pattern of racist exploitation built into the city's foundation.

"How does this make you a Mordecai heir? Liu Wei wasn't related to J. Mordecai by blood."

Anna hesitated. "This is the part that's more difficult to prove with documentation. Family oral history says that Liu Wei's daughter, my great-great-grandmother, was also J. Mordecai's daughter. That Mordecai and Liu Wei's wife had a relationship, consensual or otherwise, and that she bore Mordecai's child."

"But she was raised as Liu Wei's daughter?"

"Yes. Liu Wei accepted her as his own, whether out of love, honor, or to maintain the business partnership, I don't know. But family stories have always said that my great-great-grandmother had white heritage, that she was half-white, half-Chinese. As a child, I thought it was just genetic variation. When I started researching Liu Wei's connection to Mordecai, I realized the truth might be more complicated."

"Do you have DNA evidence?"

"I have my own DNA test results showing European ancestry, which is consistent with having a white great-great-great-grandfather. But I don't have Mordecai family DNA to compare it against. If I could get a sample from confirmed Mordecai descendants, like Thomas Crane, I could prove the connection definitively."

Elena considered this. Anna's claim was both more complex and more difficult to verify than the others. It involved a hidden business partnership, a mixed-race child, and racist historical erasure. All of which were plausible given the era's social dynamics, but none of which had clear documentation.

"Ms. Reed mentioned that David Mordecai's will requires claimants to answer questions about family history that only true heirs should know. Have you been given those questions?"

"Yes. And I answered them all correctly. David Mordecai apparently had some of Liu Wei's family records in his possession. He knew about the Chinese connection. That's partly why he excluded Thomas Crane from the inheritance. Thomas participated in the Guardians conspiracy, the same conspiracy that stole from Liu Wei and erased his contribution to the city."

"So David Mordecai was trying to correct historical wrongs by directing the inheritance to different branches of the family?"

"That's my interpretation. He wanted heirs who understood the full truth, who acknowledged all the victims of the founding conspiracy, not just the ones who fit convenient narratives."

After Anna left, Elena sat with Catherine Reed, discussing the three claims.

"What's your assessment?" Catherine asked.

"Sarah Mordecai's claim appears straightforward. Illegitimate son, documented lineage, no glaring inconsistencies. Richard Grant's claim is more suspicious. Hidden twin daughter, convenient documentation appearing after a century of silence. Anna Chen's claim is the most complex. Mixed-race child, hidden business partnership, family maintained through Chinese American community rather than mainstream society."

"Which claims do you think are legitimate?"

"I don't know yet. I need to verify the documentation, probably require DNA testing, dig deeper into the historical records. But here's what troubles me: David Mordecai's will specifically excluded Thomas Crane. That suggests David knew about other heirs and wanted to direct the inheritance away from the Guardians conspiracy line. Anna's story about Liu Wei's partnership and the Chinese heritage would explain why David knew details that contradicted mainstream history."

"So you think Anna's claim might be legitimate?"

"I think it's worth investigating thoroughly. The others too. But I also think there's something we're missing. An inheritance hunt designed by an elderly man who included cryptic requirements? That sounds like someone who knew the truth would be contested and wanted to make sure the right people received the money."

Over the next several days, Elena dug into the three claims exhaustively. She hired forensic document examiners to analyze birth certificates, letters, and family records. She consulted with genealogists about the feasibility of each family tree. She researched historical records of Chinese involvement in Pacific Northwest commerce.

What she found was troubling.

Sarah Mordecai's documents appeared authentic, properly aged, consistent with historical records. Her family tree was plausible, her story consistent. But Elena found something odd: several of the documents in Sarah's collection had been purchased from historical document dealers within the past two years. The dates and names had been authentic eighteenth-century documents, but someone had added the Mordecai family connections recently using period-appropriate ink and techniques.

Sophisticated forgery, but forgery nonetheless.

Richard Grant's documents were even more suspicious. The birth certificate for Elizabeth Mordecai appeared to be authentic paper from the correct era, but the ink showed signs of accelerated aging. Someone had created a fake birth certificate using genuine old paper, artificially aged it, and presented it as historical fact. The other documents in Richard's collection showed similar red flags.

But Anna Chen's documents, the Chinese ledgers and contracts, were genuine. Elena had them examined by experts in nineteenth-century Chinese calligraphy and business practices. They were authentic records from Liu Wei's trading company, documenting a partnership with someone identified only as "Mo-de-kai" – a phonetic rendering of Mordecai in Chinese characters.

The business relationship was real. The financial investment was real. Liu Wei had indeed been J. Mordecai's hidden partner, erased from history by racist exclusion.

But that didn't prove Anna was descended from Mordecai. It proved her ancestor had been business partners with Mordecai and had been cheated out of money. The biological connection remained unproven.

Elena called Marcus Chen to discuss her findings. "Your cousin's historical research is legitimate. Liu Wei really was Mordecai's partner, really did invest in Ashmont Bay's founding, really was erased from the historical record. But I can't prove she's biologically descended from Mordecai without DNA testing."

"Can't she just get tested against Thomas Crane? That would settle it."

"I asked. Thomas Crane has refused to provide DNA samples, claiming it's an invasion of privacy. Which is convenient for him if he's worried other heirs might displace him."

"What about the other claimants? Sarah and Richard?"

"Both are frauds. Sophisticated frauds with expensive forgeries, but frauds nonetheless. They heard about the inheritance, fabricated genealogies, and hoped no one would look too closely."

"So Anna might be the only legitimate heir?"

"Or there are no legitimate heirs beyond Thomas Crane, and David Mordecai's will was based on false information. I need to dig deeper."

Elena spent the next week researching David Mordecai himself. Who was he? How had he known about Liu Wei's partnership? Why had he structured his will the way he did?

What she discovered was that David Mordecai had been a meticulous researcher, almost obsessive. His apartment, which Catherine Reed allowed Elena to examine, was filled with historical documents, genealogical charts, boxes of correspondence. David had spent decades researching the Mordecai family history, trying to understand what had happened to his ancestor and the fortune that had been stolen.

And he had discovered Liu Wei's partnership. David's notes showed that he had tracked down Chinese language documents, had interviewed descendants of Liu Wei's business associates, had pieced together the hidden history of Chinese investment in Ashmont Bay.

More than that, David had been corresponding with Liu Wei's descendants. Elena found letters between David and Anna Chen going back three years. They had been researching together, comparing notes, building a collaborative picture of their intertwined family histories.

Anna had known about the inheritance. She and David had been working together to document the hidden Chinese connection to the Mordecai fortune.

Which meant Anna hadn't just stumbled into this inheritance claim. She had been cultivating David, positioning herself to inherit, possibly manipulating an elderly man into believing they were related when they might not be.

Elena confronted Anna at her university office. The graduate student looked exhausted, surrounded by stacks of books and research papers.

"You lied to me," Elena said without preamble. "You said you only recently discovered your connection to David Mordecai. But you've been corresponding with him for three years. You helped him research the family history. You knew about the inheritance long before he died."

Anna's composure cracked. "I didn't lie. I said I discovered my connection to J. Mordecai through my research. That's true. I met David three years ago when I was researching Chinese merchants in Pacific Northwest history. We connected over our shared interest in the Mordecai family. He became a mentor to me."

"A mentor who was planning to leave you forty million dollars?"

"I didn't know about the will until after he died! David never told me he was planning to leave me anything. We just worked together on the research because we both wanted the truth about our families to be known."

"Then why did you hide your relationship with him? Why not mention it immediately?"

"Because I knew how it would look. I knew people would think I had manipulated him, that I was a gold digger or a con artist. But I genuinely cared about David. He was brilliant and kind and obsessed with historical justice. His death broke my heart."

Elena studied Anna carefully, looking for deception. She saw grief, frustration, and genuine emotion. But she also saw a graduate student who would have very good reason to cultivate a relationship with a wealthy elderly man researching the same obscure historical topic.

"Anna, I need to ask you directly. Did you manipulate David Mordecai into believing you were his relative in order to inherit his money?"

"No! I showed him the family documents, the same ones I showed you. He drew his own conclusions about whether we were related by blood. I never claimed biological descent until after his death, when the will was read and I realized what he had believed."

"So you're not sure you're actually descended from J. Mordecai?"

"I'm not sure. The family stories say my great-great-grandmother was Mordecai's daughter, but I don't have definitive proof. David believed it was true based on the historical context and the family records. I think it might be true. But I'm not certain."

"Then you might be claiming an inheritance you have no right to."

"Or I might be finally getting compensation for what my family was cheated out of over a century ago. Liu Wei invested in Ashmont Bay, was promised a share of the profits, and received nothing. His descendants deserve restitution, whether or not we're biologically related to Mordecai."

Elena saw the argument clearly. Anna was positioning the inheritance as historical restitution for racist theft, not as biological descent. It was morally compelling but legally complicated.

That evening, Elena met with Catherine Reed to deliver her preliminary report.

"Sarah Mordecai and Richard Grant are frauds. Their documentation is forged. They should be eliminated from consideration and possibly prosecuted for attempted fraud."

"And Anna Chen?"

"Her historical research is legitimate. Liu Wei really was Mordecai's partner and was really cheated. But her biological descent from J. Mordecai is unproven and might be unprovable without DNA testing. Whether that makes her a legitimate heir under David Mordecai's will depends on how you interpret his intentions."

Catherine considered this. "David's will says the inheritance should go to 'true heirs who have honored the Mordecai name.' Anna has certainly honored the Mordecai name by researching the history and exposing the truth about Liu Wei's partnership. But is she a true heir?"

"That's a question for a judge, not an investigator. I can provide the facts. The legal interpretation is up to others."

"What do you think David intended?"

Elena thought about the elderly man obsessed with historical justice, corresponding with Anna, researching the hidden Chinese connection. "I think David knew exactly what he was doing. I think he structured his will to direct the inheritance to someone who would use it to continue fighting for historical justice, to expose the wrongs that were never corrected. Anna fits that description, whether or not she's biologically descended from J. Mordecai."

"So you think she should inherit?"

"I think David did. Whether that's legally sufficient is another question."

Catherine nodded slowly. "There's one more thing you should know. Thomas Crane has filed a legal challenge to David Mordecai's will. He's claiming that the provisions excluding him are discriminatory and legally unenforceable. He's arguing that as the confirmed biological descendant of J. Mordecai, he should inherit regardless of David's personal feelings about the Guardians conspiracy."

"Is he likely to succeed?"

"Possibly. Courts are generally reluctant to enforce provisions in wills that discriminate based on moral judgments about heirs' conduct. Thomas has a strong legal argument."

Which meant the inheritance was about to become a legal battle, not just a genealogical investigation. Thomas Crane, convicted conspirator and confirmed Mordecai heir, versus Anna Chen, historical researcher with an unproven biological connection but a strong moral claim.

And somewhere in the mess, the truth about the Mordecai family and their legacy, waiting to be either exposed or buried once again.

Elena left the law offices and walked through downtown Seattle, thinking about justice and restitution, biological descent and moral worth. Adelaide Thorne's case had been about exposing a century-old conspiracy. This case was about who deserved to benefit from correcting that wrong.

Her phone buzzed with a text from an unknown number: "Stop investigating the Mordecai inheritance. Anna Chen is dangerous. She killed David Mordecai to steal his research and position herself as his heir. If you keep digging, you'll be next."

Elena stared at the message, her heart sinking. Just when she thought the case was about genealogy and legal arguments, someone was claiming murder.

Again.

She called Detective Chen. "We need to talk. I think I have another body."
